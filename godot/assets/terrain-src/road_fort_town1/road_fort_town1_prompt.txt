SUBJECT: A small walled TOWN, drawn as ONE compact block that stands upright and
faces the viewer, the way a piece stands on a game board.

A low drystone wall of rough grey fieldstone runs across the FRONT of the block,
with one simple timber gate in the middle of it. Behind that wall, and rising
well above it, stand six to eight squat cottages of the same rough grey
fieldstone, packed shoulder to shoulder at varied sizes and slightly varied
angles — never in a row — with one taller hall near the middle standing head and
shoulders above the rest. Every building has a steep pitched roof of slate laid
over timber beams.

THE ROOFS are the most important shapes in the picture: three to five of them
large and clearly readable, overlapping each other as the buildings step back, so
that the town reads as a solid mass and not as a flat wall. The whole roofscape
must be plainly visible from this angle — the game recolours these roofs to show
who holds the town.

THE FOOT — the base of the drystone wall is a clean, straight, level line across
the bottom of the object. That line is where the game sets the town on the
ground. Draw nothing below it.

SIZE AND SHAPE — a chunky block, about as wide as it is tall and never more than
1.3 times as wide as tall. It is the only object in the picture; it sits in the
middle with clear space on all four sides and touches no edge.

COLOR — the stone is plain grey with almost no colour in it, neither warm nor
cool. The slate roofs are neutral grey: base tone #6E6D6B, with a lighter
#9B9995 where the light catches the slope and a darker #4D4C4A in shadow. Hold
that roof colour exactly — later pictures in this set have to match it. Only the
gate, the doors and the roof timbers are weathered brown. NO ochre, NO warm tan,
and NO strong red, blue or green anywhere: those colours mark who holds the town.

NOTHING ELSE — no ground, no grass, no soil, no paving and no cobbles under or
around the wall; no people, no animals, no carts, no barrels, no flags, no
signage, no smoke, no fire. Read at thumbnail size: a few large legible shapes,
no fine detail, no picked-out windows.
