STYLE: A map tile texture for a fantasy tactics game, in the same clean
stylized cel-shaded look as the game's terrain and unit art: bold flat shading,
clear dark outlines, a mature, slightly muted, limited color palette (NOT bright
saturated, NOT painterly photorealism). Drawn like the world map of a classic 2D
role-playing game, seen from DIRECTLY OVERHEAD — flat, no perspective, no
horizon, no sky, no depth, nothing standing up. Read at thumbnail size: a few
large legible shapes, no fine detail. Square 1:1.

SUBJECT: The stone-paved roadway of an old bridge, seen from straight above —
just the paving, filling the whole picture.

FILL THE FRAME — IMPORTANT: the stone paving covers the ENTIRE square, edge to
edge, corner to corner. This is a texture swatch, not a scene and not an object.
NO background, NO grass, NO water, NO river, NO bank, NO parapet, NO railing, NO
kerb, NO edge, NO border, NO frame, NO vignette, NO rounded corners. The paving
runs off all four sides. Nothing stands on it and nothing lies on it.

THE STONES: irregular flagstones fitted together, hand-laid by a mason, each one
a slightly different size and a slightly different shape — some four-sided, some
five- or six-sided, a few small ones wedged between big ones. A stone is roughly
a fifteenth of the frame across, and none is bigger than a tenth of the frame.

NO PATTERN — CRITICAL: this same texture will be used for every stone tile on
the map, unrotated, so any regularity shows up immediately as a repeat. There
must be NO rows, NO courses, NO grid, NO brickwork or running bond, NO
herringbone, NO fan or circular pattern, NO radiating layout, and NO joint line
that runs straight across more than about a fifth of the frame. The stones sit
at all angles and their joints wander. Nothing anywhere in the picture is big
enough or distinctive enough to be recognised as a landmark if this square were
laid down twenty times side by side.

THE JOINTS: the gaps between stones are THIN and DARK — narrow dark lines, a
little darker than the stone itself. CRITICAL: no white or pale joints, no wide
mortar beds, no bright grout. The joints must never become the loudest thing in
the picture.

COLOR: dry grey stone, roughly hex #8C8A82 — a calm, slightly warm mid grey,
almost colourless, clearly darker than the game's grass (#B4C6A0) and its dirt
roads (#D8C39A) and plainly different from its blue water. Each stone is a
slightly different shade of that grey, some a touch warmer, some a touch cooler,
scattered unevenly so the variation never falls into a pattern. NO stone may be
white or near-white, and none may be near-black. NO moss, NO grass in the
joints, NO puddles, NO snow, NO sand, NO leaves, NO rubble, NO cracks big enough
to notice, NO carved marks, NO inscriptions, NO wheel ruts.

Lit dimly and evenly from straight above, with NO cast shadow at all and no
light direction: the stones are flat paving and throw no shadows. Shading stays
inside each stone — very slight, just enough to tell one stone from the next.
