SUBJECT: The INSIDE of the same stone TOWN, seen from the street — a WIDE BAND
of backdrop that stands behind the fighting in a battle window. It is not a
single object standing in the middle of the frame; it is a stretch of one street
seen close, running off the left edge of the picture.

THERE IS NO TOWN WALL AND NO GATE in this picture. The battle happens inside the
town, so what stands behind the soldiers is the houses that line the street.

WHAT IS IN IT — a row of squat cottages of rough grey fieldstone, packed
shoulder to shoulder along the far side of a street, their front walls facing
the viewer. Each has a steep pitched roof of slate laid over timber beams, a
plain timber door, and one or two small shuttered windows. Heights and widths
vary and the fronts step slightly in and out — never a flat even row. One taller
hall stands head and shoulders above the rest.

THE LANE — a narrow lane runs back between the houses into the distance, its
mouth about a third of the way in from the RIGHT end of the picture. Inside the
lane the walls close in and the buildings get smaller and darker as they recede,
so the town plainly continues past this row. This gap is what makes the picture
read as a street rather than a solid wall of buildings. The floor of the lane
may be seen inside the gap; nowhere else is any ground drawn.

THE ROOFS are the most important shapes: three to five of them large and clearly
readable, overlapping each other as the houses step back. The whole roofscape
must be plainly visible from this angle — the game recolours these roofs to show
who holds the town. Nothing pokes above the top of the band; leave a little
clear space over the highest roof.

THE TWO ENDS ARE DIFFERENT — this is the most important thing.
- LEFT end: the row of houses runs straight off the edge of the picture and is
  cut by it. Nothing tapers, nothing ends, nothing is rounded off there.
- RIGHT end: the town has to finish inside the picture. Finish it honestly: the
  last house turns its corner and the row runs away from the viewer, the
  buildings behind it getting smaller and lower as they recede, and the last of
  them fading out. Do NOT stop the row in mid-air with a raw broken end.

SHAPE AND PLACE IN THE FRAME — the picture is square. The band of town fills the
BOTTOM HALF of it, edge to edge: twice as wide as it is tall. In a 1024-pixel
picture the town is about 512 pixels tall and 1024 pixels wide. It touches the
left edge, the right edge and the bottom edge. Everything above it is background
(see below).

THE BOTTOM EDGE — the front walls of the houses are cut flat by the bottom edge
of the picture. Draw nothing below them: no street surface, no cobbles, no
paving, no ground, no grass, no soil, no cast shadow. The walls must run all the
way down to the very bottom edge and be cut by it — no strip of background along
the bottom. Soldiers will stand in front of the houses and their heads will
overlap the bottom of the walls.

VIEWING ANGLE — seen from a slight high three-quarter angle, as if looking down
onto the street from above and in front, but much closer than a map: the front
walls, the doors and the mouth of the lane are clearly readable, and the roofs
are still fully in view above them.

COLOR — the stone is plain grey with almost no colour in it, neither warm nor
cool. The slate roofs are the SAME neutral grey as the town on the map piece:
base tone #6E6D6B, lighter #9B9995 where the light catches the slope, darker
#4D4C4A in shadow. Match those numbers. Only the doors, the shutters and the
roof timbers are weathered brown. NO ochre, NO warm tan, and NO strong red, blue
or green anywhere: those colours mark who holds the town.

THE BACKGROUND — everything above the roofline is PURE FLAT WHITE, #FFFFFF, with
NO texture, NO shading, NO gradient, NO sky, NO clouds. This white is going to be
deleted, so the roofline must be a hard, clean, crisp edge against it. Nothing in
the town may be white or near-white.

NOTHING ELSE — no people, no animals, no carts, no barrels, no flags, no
signage, no smoke, no fire, no birds, no washing lines, no market stalls.

This is a near view, so a little more detail than the map piece is welcome — the
courses of the stonework, the boards of a door, the ridge line of a roof — but
keep it in bold flat shapes, with no fine texture that would fight with the
figures standing in front of it.
