STYLE: A side-on falling-water texture for a fantasy tactics game, in the same
clean stylized cel-shaded look as the game's terrain and unit art: bold flat
shading, a mature, slightly muted, limited colour palette (NOT bright saturated,
NOT painterly photorealism). Viewed straight from the FRONT, face-on — a flat lay
with NO perspective, NO horizon, NO sky, NO ground line, NO floor. The texture
fills the ENTIRE square frame edge to edge, with NO border, NO frame, NO
vignette, and no single big focal object. It must tile SEAMLESSLY LEFT TO RIGHT:
the left and right edges continue into each other. CRITICAL: vary the water
RANDOMLY and UNEVENLY — never a repeated motif, never the same shape twice, never
in columns of equal width. Avoid large, high-contrast patches (they repeat visibly
when tiled); keep large-scale colour even and low-contrast. Square 1:1.

SUBJECT: The face of a WATERFALL — an unbroken curtain of river water pouring
straight DOWN, seen face-on. Nothing but falling water across the whole frame: NO
rock, NO cliff, NO stone, NO ledge, NO moss, NO plants, NO spray cloud, NO
rainbow, NO objects of any kind, and nothing visible behind the water.

The water falls in tall vertical threads and ribbons that run the full height of
the frame. They differ in width and brightness, wander slightly, split and rejoin,
and are spaced unevenly — never straight parallel lines, never evenly spaced,
never columns of equal width. Between them the water is a smoother, slightly
deeper sheet, so the relief reads as clearly VERTICAL overall. A few short torn
edges and small flecks of white spray sit along the threads, small and scattered.

BOTH the top and the bottom edges are cut off FLAT where the frame ends — this is
the middle of the fall. NO lip, NO crest, NO brink, NO still water above; NO
plunge pool, NO churn, NO foam bank, NO mist, NO splash below. The fall is the
same in character from top to bottom, so it still reads right when it is stretched
tall or squeezed short.

COLOUR: the same river blue as the water above, hex #63A0D6, run a shade deeper in
the sheet between the threads. The threads themselves are a pale, light version of
that same blue, and the small spray flecks may reach near-white — but the frame
overall must stay clearly BLUE. Do NOT push it toward green or teal, do NOT grey
it down, and do NOT wash the whole frame out to white or pale grey.

Shade in FLAT tones — a light thread, a mid sheet, a deep shadow between — flat
cel shading of the water's own forms, NOT a cast shadow and NOT a light direction,
and no tone near-black.

Read at thumbnail size: the fall is only a narrow strip on screen, so keep the
threads CHUNKY and few — about eight to twelve of them across the width of the
frame — with no fine detail that would disappear. Spread the relief evenly from
top to bottom, with no single large feature that sits at one height only.
