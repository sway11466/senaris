DESIGN — the town's guard post. This block is repeated word for word in every slot
of this skin (the board object, the two captured colours, the combat band), so
that the small piece on the board and the big building in the battle window are
plainly the same place.

- ONE single-storey building, no more. Walls of the same rough grey fieldstone as
  the town's houses, #8C8A82, laid without courses; the gable carries exposed
  timber framing in weathered brown, #6E5A42.
- Its ROOF is the biggest shape in the picture: one steep gabled slate roof,
  taller and broader than a cottage's, unbroken from ridge to eaves. Neutral
  slate: base tone #6E6D6B, catch-light #9B9995 on the lit slope, shadow #4D4C4A.
  Hold those numbers exactly — the game recolours this roof to show who holds the
  post, and the later pictures in this set have to match.
- The roof is ONE flat colour per slope: NO stripes, NO chequer, NO tile picked
  out light and dark, NO patch of another material, and nothing sitting on it.
  The only shading allowed is the two slopes differing slightly.
- A stout timber double door stands in the middle of the front wall, with one
  small shuttered window beside it.
- In FRONT of the building, a LOW stone wall about waist high runs to the left and
  to the right, closing a small yard. It ENDS inside the picture at both sides
  with a plain squared-off stub — it never runs off the frame. A simple timber
  gate stands open in the middle of it.
- Leaning against the front wall of the building, a RACK OF SPEARS: a plain timber
  frame with three or four spears in it. This is the one thing that says soldiers
  keep this place.
- NEVER: a tower, a belfry, an upper storey, a portcullis, a drawbridge, a moat,
  a town wall running off the picture, a second building, a market stall, a cart,
  barrels, banners, flags, torches, people, animals, smoke.
- It must be tellable apart from the two other town pieces at a glance: the town's
  FORT is a cluster of six to eight buildings with a tall hall behind a high wall;
  an ordinary town BLOCK is four or five cottages with no wall and no yard. This
  is ONE building with a low walled yard and a spear rack.

SUBJECT: That guard post, drawn as a single object that stands upright the way a
piece stands on a game board, cut out and set down on the game's own road.

SQUARE-ON — CRITICAL. The building and the wall are seen STRAIGHT FROM THE FRONT
and only a little from above, exactly as the town's houses are drawn: the front
wall is a plain rectangle square to the picture, the gable above it is an upright
triangle, and the roof shows as a band of slate above them with its ridge running
level. NO corner points at you. NO side wall, NO end wall and NO receding edge is
visible. This is NOT an isometric view, NOT a three-quarter view, NOT a
perspective view.

DEPTH — the low yard wall stands in FRONT of the building: draw it lower down in
the picture and let it overlap the bottom of the building's wall, so the two read
as one mass with the yard between them. That is the whole depth; the yard floor
is not drawn.

THE FOOT — the bottom of the yard wall sits on ONE straight level line across the
bottom of the picture. That line is where the game sets the post on the ground.
Draw nothing below it: no ground, no grass, no paving, no step, no shadow.

SIZE AND SHAPE — about 1.4 times as wide as it is tall, counting the yard wall.
It stands among the houses of a town and should read as one notch bigger and
taller than a single cottage, but far smaller than the town's fort. The post is
the only object in the picture, sits in the middle, and touches no edge — leave
clear white on all four sides.

THE BACKGROUND is PURE FLAT WHITE, #FFFFFF, with NO texture, NO shading, NO
gradient, NO speckle, NO grey, NO drop shadow. The white is deleted and the game
draws its own road under the post, so the edge against the white must be hard and
clean.

NO LIGHT DIRECTION — no wall has a bright side and a dark side, and nothing casts
a shadow onto anything else: none under the eaves, none from the yard wall onto
the building, none on the ground. Shading stays inside a surface. Read at
thumbnail size: a few large legible shapes, no fine detail, no picked-out stones.
