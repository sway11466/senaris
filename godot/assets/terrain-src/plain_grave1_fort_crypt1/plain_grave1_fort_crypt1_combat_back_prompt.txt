SUBJECT: The same stone CRYPT as the map piece, seen CLOSE — a WIDE BAND of
backdrop that stands behind the fighting in a battle window. It is not a single
object floating in the middle of the frame; it is a stretch of the graveyard
seen near, running off the LEFT edge of the picture.

WHAT IS IN IT — the crypt stands a little LEFT of the middle of the band, the
largest thing in the picture. It is one squat burial vault of rough grey
blockwork: a single low chamber, plainly wider than it is tall, with a heavy
pitched stone roof set straight on top of it and thick corner blocks. It has NO
tower, NO bell, NO cross and NO spire — it is a place the dead are put, not a
church.

THE ENTRANCE is the point of the picture: a plain arched DOORWAY in the front
wall, standing OPEN with no door in it, reading as a hole that goes back under
the ground. Three or four worn steps run down from it, and the stone at the
sides of the opening is chipped and shifted.

A DIM COLD LIGHT comes up out of that hole. The top and the back of the opening
are near-black, hex #20211F, and lower down, toward the threshold, the darkness
turns to a dull blue-green: around hex #3A5A54, reaching hex #4F6E66 at its
brightest just above the sill. It is faint and sickly, not a lamp and not a
fire — a cold shine from something further down. Keep it INSIDE the opening and
on the sill only: it does NOT spill onto the steps, the walls, the ground or the
markers, and it casts NO glow, halo or bloom outside the doorway. The opening is
otherwise plainly EMPTY — no eyes, no face, no creature, no mist, no smoke,
nothing standing in it. Whatever comes out of this place is not in the picture.

THE ROOF must be plainly visible from this angle, big and simple, because the
game recolours it to show who holds the crypt.

AROUND IT — old grave markers stand in the ground on both sides of the crypt:
plain weathered slabs with rounded tops, short crosses with thick stubby arms,
snapped stubs, each leaning at its own angle, scattered irregularly and never
in a row. They are much lower than the crypt — the tallest reaches about a
third of its height.

THE TWO ENDS ARE DIFFERENT — this is the most important thing.
- LEFT end: the graveyard runs straight off the edge of the picture and is cut
  by it. The markers nearest the left edge are cut through by the frame.
  Nothing tapers, nothing ends, nothing is rounded off there.
- RIGHT end: the graveyard has to finish inside the picture. Finish it
  honestly: past the crypt the markers get smaller and lower as the ground
  recedes away from the viewer, and the last of them fade out. Do NOT stop the
  row in mid-air with a raw broken end, and do NOT put a wall, fence, hedge,
  kerb or gate there — there is no boundary anywhere in this picture.

SHAPE AND PLACE IN THE FRAME — the picture is square. The crypt and the markers
sit in a WIDE LOW BAND across the middle of it: in a 1024-pixel picture the
crypt is about 380 pixels tall to the ridge of its roof and about 520 pixels
wide, and everything else is lower. The ONLY edge of the picture that may cut
anything is the LEFT one. Nothing touches the top edge, the right edge or the
bottom edge.

THERE IS NO GROUND IN THIS PICTURE — this is the rule most easily broken, so
read it twice. Do NOT draw a band of earth, a strip of soil, a ground plane, a
horizon line, a patch of colour or a shelf of any kind under the buildings. The
crypt, the steps and every marker stand DIRECTLY ON THE WHITE: each one simply
ends at its own base with a hard, clean outline, and below that base there is
nothing but #FFFFFF. No grass, no tufts, no pebbles, no dirt, no plinth, no
foundation.

AND NOTHING CASTS A SHADOW ONTO THE WHITE. The crypt casts no shadow, the steps
cast no shadow, no marker casts a shadow — not a soft one, not a faint one, not
a contact shadow where stone meets background. The shading ON the stone itself
stays (the shadowed side wall, the dark inside the doorway, the shaded faces of
the markers); only what would fall on the background is absent. The white must
be one flat value everywhere.

VIEWING ANGLE — seen from the front and slightly above, much closer than the
map piece: the front wall, the open doorway and the steps are all clearly
readable, and the whole roof is still in view above them.

COLOR — the crypt's stone is a dull grey, rougher and darker than dressed
masonry: base tone #948F80 where the light catches it, #7C796C in the mass,
#575142 in shadow. The doorway is near-black #20211F at the back, going to the
dull blue-green described above near the sill. The grave markers are a
weathered grey-green, hex #8A8676. The ROOF is
plain grey stone, as colourless as the walls — base tone #7F7B70, shadowed
slopes #686458, catch-light #979386. Match those numbers: this crypt and the
map piece must read as the same building. NO ochre, NO warm tan, and NO strong
red, blue or green anywhere: those colours are what marks who holds the place,
and nobody holds it in this picture.

THE BACKGROUND — everything above the roofline is PURE FLAT WHITE, #FFFFFF,
with NO texture, NO shading, NO gradient, NO sky, NO clouds. This white is
going to be deleted, so the roofline must be a hard, clean, crisp edge against
it. Nothing in the picture may be white or near-white.

NOTHING ELSE — no people, no animals, no birds, no bones, no skulls, no
coffins, no open graves, no flowers, no candles, no lanterns, no banners, no
signage, no smoke, no fire, no lettering and no inscriptions. Apart from the
cold light inside the doorway, the picture is quiet and weathered: NO mist, NO
floating lights, NO magic circles, NO purple or green fire, NO glowing runes,
and no light anywhere else in the picture.

This is a near view, so a little more detail than the map piece is welcome —
the courses of the blockwork, the worn edges of the steps, the ridge line of
the roof — but keep it in bold flat shapes, in the same clean stylized
cel-shaded look as the game's terrain and unit art, with no fine texture that
would fight with the figures standing in front of it. Square 1:1.
