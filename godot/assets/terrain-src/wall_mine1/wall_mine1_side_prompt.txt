STYLE: A side-on wall-face texture for a fantasy tactics game, in the same clean
stylized cel-shaded look as the game's terrain and unit art: bold flat shading, a
mature, slightly muted, limited color palette (NOT bright saturated, NOT
painterly photorealism). Viewed straight from the FRONT, face-on — a flat lay
with NO perspective, NO horizon, NO sky, NO ground line, NO floor. The texture
fills the ENTIRE square frame edge to edge, with NO border, NO frame, NO
vignette. It must tile SEAMLESSLY in BOTH directions: the left and right edges
continue into each other, and the top and bottom edges continue into each other.
Keep large-scale color even and low-contrast. Square 1:1.

SUBJECT: The wall of a MINE TUNNEL dug by hand into a single mass of solid rock,
seen face-on. The rock is ONE CONTINUOUS BODY of dark cold grey-brown stone, base
color around #5F584F, its tone shifting gradually across the face, with irregular
fracture lines wandering across it. Unlike a natural cave, this face was cut by
miners: short, shallow PICK GOUGES score the rock here and there in loose,
uneven clusters, all running roughly diagonally, so it reads as worked by hand —
but still solid rock, NOT masonry, NO bricks, NO mortar lines.
Standing against the rock are exactly FOUR vertical TIMBER PROPS set at EVEN,
REGULAR intervals like a row of mine supports: their centres at one eighth, three
eighths, five eighths and seven eighths of the way across the frame, so the gap
between neighbouring posts is always the same and the rhythm continues across the
seamless left-right join. Each post is a squared-off pine timber about one
twelfth of the frame wide, clearly WOOD: a warm medium brown #8B6A47, noticeably
LIGHTER and warmer than the grey rock, with visible straight wood grain running
along its length, a few knots, and a dark outline so it stands out from the stone.
Each post runs STRAIGHT from the top edge to the bottom edge of the frame without
any end, joint, base or top, so the texture continues seamlessly upward and
downward. The four posts match in size, spacing and color; only their knots and
small cracks differ. The left and right edges of the frame are plain rock, half a gap wide each.
IRON FITTINGS: every post is wrapped by TWO dark iron bands fastened with a few
round rivet heads — one band high up, about one tenth of the way down from the
top edge, and one about halfway down. The bands are dark grey iron with a little
rust, bold enough to read at thumbnail size.
DIAGONAL BRACES, only here and there: exactly TWO diagonal timber braces, each a
squared pine timber of the same warm brown as the posts but a little narrower.
One spans the gap between the first and second posts, running from the first
post about one tenth down from the top edge down to the second post about halfway
down. The other spans the gap between the third and fourth posts, running the
opposite way, from the fourth post about one tenth down from the top edge down to
the third post about halfway down. Both braces end against the posts, fixed with
an iron plate and rivets at each end; neither touches the top or bottom edge of
the frame. The middle gap between the second and third posts has NO brace.
Lit dimly from above. NO horizontal beams, NO planks, NO other bracing. NO
vegetation of any kind — NO moss, NO plants, NO soil, NO puddles. NO objects of
any kind — NO torches, NO lamps, NO ore, NO crystals, NO rails, NO tools, NO
bones. This is only the flat wall face — NO top surface, NO floor, NO arch, NO
opening, NO cast shadow onto anything else.
