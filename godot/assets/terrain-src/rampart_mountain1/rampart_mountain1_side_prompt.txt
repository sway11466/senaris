SUBJECT: The sheer FACE of an impassable rocky ridge, seen face-on — the flank
of the same weathered rock whose crest is seen from above in the tile that goes
with this one. Viewed straight from the FRONT, NOT from overhead: a flat lay
with NO perspective, NO horizon, NO sky, NO ground line, NO floor. It must tile
SEAMLESSLY LEFT TO RIGHT: the left and right edges continue into each other.

THE ROCK: broad flat faces of bare stone, separated by irregular hairline
fissures, with chipped angular fragments wedged here and there and a few darker
recesses. It is the SAME rock as the crest: base colour hex #AA9F8D, recesses
down to about #6F6759, no face brighter than #C2B6A4 and none near-black. The
fissures run mostly DOWNWARD, as if the rock split under its own weight, but
never parallel and never in a single direction. Weathered, hard, sheer.

CRITICAL — the light comes from DIRECTLY ABOVE and from nowhere else. This
strip is wrapped around all six sides of a hexagon, so any sideways light would
contradict itself: NO light from the left, NO light from the right, NO
highlight or gradient running sideways across the frame, NO cast shadow thrown
to one side.

Read at the size it is used: the whole face is only a narrow strip on screen,
so keep the faces BROAD and few — about four to six across the width of the
frame — with no fine detail that would disappear. Spread the relief evenly from
top to bottom, keep the large-scale colour even and low-contrast, and let no
single large feature sit at one height only.

This is only the flat rock face — NO scree or rubble pile at the bottom, NO
talus slope, NO top surface, NO ground, NO cast shadow onto anything else.
