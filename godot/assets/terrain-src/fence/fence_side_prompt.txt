STYLE: A single object drawn face-on for a fantasy tactics game, in the same
clean stylized cel-shaded look as the game's terrain and unit art: bold flat
shading, clear dark outlines, a mature, slightly muted, limited color palette
(NOT bright saturated, NOT painterly photorealism). Viewed straight from the
SIDE, face-on and level with it -- a flat elevation with NO perspective, NO
foreshortening, NO horizon, NO sky, NO ground, NO floor. The object floats on a
plain PURE WHITE background that fills every part of the frame it does not
cover, including every gap inside it; that background is cut away in the game,
so it must stay clean, even and empty, with NO shadow, NO gradient, NO texture,
NO vignette. Read at thumbnail size: a few large legible shapes, no fine detail.
Square 1:1.

SUBJECT: ONE straight bay of an old wooden fence, seen from the SIDE, face-on and
level with it -- two upright posts with two horizontal rails running between
them, and nothing else.

THE SHAPE, exactly: the posts stand perfectly vertical, the rails perfectly
level, the whole bay drawn head-on across the middle of the frame with clean
empty background on all four sides. The two posts are IDENTICAL and the picture
is SYMMETRIC left to right: in the game this bay is repeated and mirrored end to
end to build a long fence, so anything that differs between the left half and the
right half turns into a visible pattern. NO gate, NO opening, NO end, NO corner,
NO diagonal brace, NO upright palings between the posts, NO wire, NO rope, NO
nails, NO sign, NO lantern, NO plants growing on it, NO objects leaning against
it.

THE PROPORTIONS: measured between the centres of the two posts, the bay is TWICE
as wide as the fence is tall. Each post is a squat square timber about one eighth
of the bay width thick, cut off flat at the top, standing a little above the top
rail. There are exactly two rails: the top rail just under the post tops, the
lower rail about halfway down. Each rail is thick -- about a fifth of the fence
height -- and runs the full way between the posts. The gap between the two rails,
and the gap below the lower rail, are LARGE and completely EMPTY: in the game the
ground and the soldiers behind the fence show through them.

THE FOOT: the two posts end flat at the bottom, both at exactly the same height,
and that line is where the fence meets the ground. The rails never reach below the
posts' feet. Below and around the feet there is NOTHING -- NO ground, NO soil, NO
grass, NO tufts, NO stones, NO shadow, no dark smear or soft dark band of any kind.

THE TIMBER: rough-sawn, dry, weathered old wood, and it must LOOK like wood even
when shrunk very small. Draw the grain BOLD and CHUNKY: a few long dark streaks
running along each rail and down each post, a dark knot or two on each rail, a
deep split at the top of each post -- big marks, cel-shaded flat, not fine
hatching and not noise (fine grain disappears when shrunk and just greys the
color out). The corners are knocked off. Keep it heavy: this must read as solid
carpentry, NOT a thin line, NOT a ladder, NOT railway track. Both posts are
weathered the same way.

COLOR: weathered BROWN timber, roughly hex #77604A -- a dry, muted, warm brown
that is unmistakably wood, NOT grey and NOT orange, and clearly darker than the
pale sage grass (#B4C6A0) it will stand on so it reads against it. Shade each
piece as ONE FLAT TONE with cel shading, two or three tones in all, between about
#8A7258 (lit) and #453626 (shaded), the grain streaks and knots a step darker
still, and outline the timber with a dark line, roughly #2A211A. No part
near-white, none near-black.

THE LIGHT comes from DIRECTLY ABOVE and from nowhere else. In the game this fence
is stood up facing all six directions and is also seen from behind (mirrored), so
any sideways light would contradict itself: NO light from the left, NO light from
the right, NO highlight or gradient running sideways, NO cast shadow anywhere --
not on the background, not from one post onto the other, not from the rails onto
anything. The only shading allowed is the top face of each piece a little lighter
and its underside a little darker, the same on both posts and along the whole
length of both rails.
