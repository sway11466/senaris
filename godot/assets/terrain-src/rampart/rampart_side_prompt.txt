STYLE: A side-on wall-face texture for a fantasy tactics game, in the same clean
stylized cel-shaded look as the game's terrain and unit art: bold flat shading, a
mature, slightly muted, limited color palette (NOT bright saturated, NOT
painterly photorealism). Viewed straight from the FRONT, face-on — a flat lay
with NO perspective, NO horizon, NO sky, NO ground line, NO floor. The texture
fills the ENTIRE square frame edge to edge, with NO border, NO frame, NO
vignette, and no single big focal object. It must tile SEAMLESSLY LEFT TO RIGHT:
the left and right edges continue into each other. Avoid large, high-contrast
patches (they repeat visibly when tiled); keep large-scale color even and
low-contrast. Square 1:1.

SUBJECT: The outer FACE of a fortress wall built of dressed ashlar masonry, seen
face-on — a human kingdom's stonework, cut and squared by masons, not a natural
rock face and not a rubble heap. The blocks are SMALL and neatly laid: a
well-built royal wall of small dressed stone, not a rough keep of boulders.

HOW MANY BLOCKS — CRITICAL, this is the point of the picture: count them. Each
course holds about FIFTEEN blocks across the width of the frame, and there are
about FOURTEEN courses from the top edge to the bottom edge. So a single block is
roughly a fifteenth of the frame wide and a fourteenth of the frame tall —
small, close to square, and there are around two hundred of them in the picture.
Do NOT draw big blocks: nothing may be as much as a sixth of the frame across.
Only a narrow horizontal strip of this picture is used on the map, so a wall face
that is drawn with a few large stones ends up as three or four huge slabs sliding
past on screen.

THE COURSES: the blocks are laid in LEVEL HORIZONTAL COURSES, one row sitting on
the next, all the way up and across. Horizontal is correct here and is the one
regularity this picture is allowed: a wall face reads the same whichever way the
wall runs. Keep every course the same height; do NOT let the rows sag, tilt,
taper or fan. Any horizontal strip cut out of this picture must show two whole
courses of block.

THE BLOCKS: within each course the blocks vary in LENGTH — most a little wider
than they are tall, a few nearly square, a short one wedged in here and there,
but none longer than twice its height. The vertical joints are STAGGERED from
course to course: never let two of them line up into a continuous vertical seam,
and never repeat the same run of block lengths twice. Corners are worn round and
faces are faintly pitted; keep the wear small and even — at this size a big chip
or a long crack reads as damage to the whole wall. Weathered but sound and
maintained — this wall still stands and is still defended.

THE JOINTS: the gaps between blocks are THIN and DARK — narrow recessed lines, a
little darker than the stone. NO white or pale mortar, NO wide mortar beds, NO
bright grout. With this many blocks the joints are the busiest thing in the
frame, so keep them fine and quiet: they must never become the loudest thing in
the picture, and the wall must still read as one grey surface from a distance.

COLOR: cool grey granite, roughly hex #7F868A — a faintly blue-grey stone, a
shade deeper than the wall's sunlit top surfaces (#8E9498 capping, #A9AEB0
walkway) because this is the shaded vertical face. Each block takes a slightly
different flat tone somewhere between #8C9296 and #6F7679, scattered unevenly so
the variation never falls into a pattern — no block near-white, none near-black,
and no run of dark blocks clumping into a patch.

CRITICAL — the light comes from DIRECTLY ABOVE and from nowhere else. This strip
is wrapped around all six sides of a hexagon, so any sideways light would
contradict itself: NO light from the left, NO light from the right, NO highlight
or gradient running sideways across the frame, NO cast shadows thrown to one
side. The upward-facing top edge of each block may catch a little light and the
underside of each block may sit a little darker — that is the only shading
allowed, and it must be the same everywhere across the width.

NOTHING ON THE WALL: NO battlements, NO merlons, NO crenellations, NO parapet,
NO coping row, NO arrow slits, NO loopholes, NO windows, NO doors, NO gate, NO
arch, NO buttress, NO tower, NO corner, NO string course, NO plinth, NO
foundation row, NO carved marks, NO inscriptions, NO banners, NO torches, NO
brackets, NO chains, NO ivy, NO moss, NO lichen, NO plants, NO soil, NO snow.
This is only the flat face of the wall — NO top surface, NO ground at the
bottom, NO shadow cast onto anything else.
