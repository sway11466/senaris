STYLE: A map tile texture for a fantasy tactics game, in the same clean
stylized cel-shaded look as the game's terrain and unit art: bold flat shading,
clear dark outlines, a mature, slightly muted, limited color palette (NOT bright
saturated, NOT painterly photorealism). Drawn like the world map of a classic 2D
role-playing game, seen from DIRECTLY OVERHEAD — flat, no perspective, no
horizon, no sky, no depth, nothing standing up. Read at thumbnail size: a few
large legible shapes, no fine detail. Square 1:1.

SUBJECT: The paved WALKWAY that runs along the top of a fortress wall — the
level stone floor the defenders walk on, seen from straight above. Just the
paving, filling the whole picture.

FILL THE FRAME — IMPORTANT: the paving covers the ENTIRE square, edge to edge,
corner to corner. This is a texture swatch, not a scene and not an object. NO
parapet, NO battlements, NO merlons, NO crenellations, NO wall, NO kerb, NO
railing, NO edge, NO steps, NO stair, NO hatch, NO background, NO grass, NO
ground, NO sky, NO border, NO frame, NO vignette, NO rounded corners. The paving
runs off all four sides. Nothing stands on it and nothing lies on it — NO
weapons, NO barrels, NO braziers, NO banners, NO people.

THE STONES: dressed paving slabs cut by a mason for a fortress — squarish and
rectangular, tidier and more squared-off than a village lane's cobbles, but not
machine-perfect: each slab a slightly different size, corners a little worn, a
few small slabs wedged between larger ones. A slab is roughly a twelfth of the
frame across, and none is bigger than an eighth of the frame.

NO PATTERN — CRITICAL: this same texture is used for every wall tile on the map,
unrotated and never mirrored, so any regularity shows up immediately as a
repeat. There must be NO rows, NO courses, NO grid, NO brickwork or running
bond, NO herringbone, NO fan or circular pattern, NO radiating layout, and NO
joint line that runs straight across more than about a fifth of the frame. The
slabs sit at slightly different angles and their joints wander. Nothing anywhere
in the picture is big enough or distinctive enough to be recognised as a
landmark if this square were laid down twenty times side by side.

THE JOINTS: the gaps between slabs are THIN and DARK — narrow dark lines, a
little darker than the stone itself. CRITICAL: no white or pale joints, no wide
mortar beds, no bright grout. The joints must never become the loudest thing in
the picture.

COLOR: pale cool grey granite, roughly hex #A9AEB0 — a light, faintly blue-grey
stone, almost colourless. It must read as the LIGHTER of the two stones that
meet inside one map tile: clearly lighter than the wall's parapet capping
(#8E9498) that borders it, and clearly lighter and cooler than the game's stone
road (#8C8A82). Also plainly different from the graveyard rubble's warm grey-tan
(#B9B2A4) and from the game's grass (#B4C6A0). Each slab is a slightly different
shade of that grey, some a touch warmer, some a touch cooler, scattered unevenly
so the variation never falls into a pattern. NO slab may be white or near-white,
and none may be near-black. Swept and walked-on: NO moss, NO lichen, NO grass in
the joints, NO puddles, NO snow, NO sand, NO leaves, NO rubble, NO dirt patches,
NO cracks big enough to notice, NO carved marks, NO inscriptions, NO blood.

Lit dimly and evenly from straight above, with NO cast shadow at all and no
light direction: the slabs are flat paving and throw no shadows. Shading stays
inside each slab — very slight, just enough to tell one slab from the next.
