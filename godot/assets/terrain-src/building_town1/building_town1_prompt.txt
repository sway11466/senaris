DESIGN — the town's houses. This block is repeated word for word in every slot
of this skin (the board object and the combat band), so that the small clump on
the board and the big houses in the battle window are plainly the same town.

- Every house is a single-storey cottage with a steep gabled roof. The roof is
  as tall as the wall beneath it: half the height of the house is roof.
- Walls are rough grey fieldstone, #8C8A82, laid without courses, with paler
  mortar between the stones. The triangular gable carries exposed timber framing
  in weathered brown, #6E5A42 — three or four straight members, no carving, no
  curves.
- EVERY roof is grey slate and nothing else: base tone #7E8388, each house a
  slightly different shade of that same grey, some a touch lighter, some a touch
  darker. NO red, NO terracotta, NO orange, NO brown, NO ochre, NO blue, NO
  green, NO thatch, NO straw, NO reed, NO wooden shingles, NO turf. Red and blue
  roofs mark who holds a fortified place in this game, so an ordinary house may
  never wear them.
- Each roof has a plain straight ridge and eaves that overhang the wall by about
  a tenth of the house's width. No dormers, no gables added on the slope, no
  tiles picked out one by one.
- One plain plank door per house, weathered brown #6E5A42. Windows are small
  squares with shutters, at most two to a house, no glass and no lattice.
- About half the houses carry a square stone chimney at the ridge.
- NEVER in this skin: a tower, a gate, a town wall, a church, a belfry, an
  arcade, a shop front, a sign, a flag, a banner. Those belong to the town's
  fort, which has to stay tellable apart from ordinary houses.
- NEVER: people, animals, carts, barrels, crates, washing lines, smoke, fire.

SUBJECT: ONE clump of four or five of those cottages, standing in TWO RANKS —
some near, some further back — drawn as a single object that stands upright the
way a piece stands on a game board. This is one corner of a town, not a whole
town: no wall around it, no gate, no square, no street drawn under it.

SQUARE-ON — CRITICAL. Every house is seen STRAIGHT FROM THE FRONT, like a flat
elevation drawing: the wall facing you is a plain rectangle, square to the
picture, and the gable above it is an upright triangle. NO house is turned so
that a corner points at you. NO side wall, NO end wall and NO receding edge is
visible on any house. This is NOT an isometric view, NOT a three-quarter view,
NOT a perspective view. The only thing seen from above is the roof itself, and
only a little: each roof shows as a shallow band of slate above its wall, its
ridge running level across the picture.

THE TWO RANKS — depth is made by HEIGHT ON THE PAGE AND SIZE, never by turning a
house.

- The NEAR rank is one or two cottages standing on the bottom line of the
  picture. They are the largest houses in the clump and they sit in the middle
  of the width, spanning only about six tenths of it — narrow, because the near
  edge of the hexagonal tile they stand on is narrow.
- The FAR rank is three cottages whose wall bottoms are drawn about a quarter of
  the picture's height ABOVE the bottom line, so they read as standing further
  back. They are drawn ten to twenty per cent smaller than the near ones, and
  they spread across the FULL width of the clump, wider than the near rank,
  because the tile is at its widest through the middle.
- The far rank is partly hidden: the near cottages overlap the bottoms of the
  far walls, so the two ranks read as one mass and the ground between them is
  never shown.
- That is the whole depth. NO third rank, no houses shrinking away into the
  distance, no street running back between them.

THE SILHOUETTE is therefore a low diamond: narrow along the bottom, widest
across the middle, and closing again at the ridges of the far roofs. Neighbouring
clumps interlock like brickwork when this shape is laid across the map, so do
not square the outline off.

THE FOOT — the bottoms of the NEAR walls sit on ONE straight level line across
the bottom of the picture. That line is where the game sets the clump on the
ground. Draw nothing below it: no ground, no grass, no paving, no step, no
shadow. The far rank's raised bases are part of the picture and stay well above
that line.

SIZE AND SHAPE — low and wide: about 1.8 times as wide as it is tall, and never
taller than 0.55 of its width. It has to sit inside one hexagon of the map
without hiding the tiles behind it, so height is the thing to hold down. The
clump is the only object in the picture, sits in the middle, and touches no edge
— leave clear white on all four sides.

THE BACKGROUND is PURE FLAT WHITE, #FFFFFF, with NO texture, NO shading, NO
gradient, NO speckle, NO grey, NO drop shadow. The white is deleted and the game
draws its own road under the clump, so the edge against the white must be hard
and clean.

NO LIGHT DIRECTION — no house has a bright side and a dark side, and no house
casts a shadow onto the house beside it. Shading stays inside a surface: the
roof is a shade darker than the wall, and that is all. Read at thumbnail size: a
few large legible shapes, no fine detail, no picked-out stones.
