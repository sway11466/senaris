DESIGN — the town's kitchen garden. This block is repeated word for word in every
slot of this skin, so that the little plot on the board and any later close-up in
the battle window are plainly the same garden.

- The plot is FLAT GROUND: turned soil, dark brown #4E3B2A, level with the road
  around it. It has NO THICKNESS — no slab of earth, no raised bed, no cut face
  of soil along the front, no step down to the road, and no edging of any kind
  (no plank, no board, no kerb, no stones, no box, no frame). The soil simply
  ends where the plot ends, in a soft slightly irregular line.
- The soil is worked into three or four low ridges running left to right.
- BEAN POLES are the shape that has to read from far away: THREE A-frames of
  peeled sticks, each one two poles crossed and tied near the top, standing along
  the plot at slightly different sizes and spacings. Bean vines wind up them, and
  a few leaves hang off the vines, so each A-frame reads as a leafy triangle
  rather than as bare sticks.
- Vegetables grow in the rows: round cabbages, onion tops, carrot tops, in a
  muted mid green #7E9660, one kind to a ridge. Bare soil shows between them.
- NEVER: a fence, a hedge, a wall, a gate, a trellis panel, a shed, a scarecrow,
  a well, a water butt, a cart, tools, baskets, flowers, fruit, people, animals.
  A fence is its own terrain in this game and must never appear here.

SUBJECT: ONE such kitchen garden, drawn as a single object that stands on the
board the way a piece stands on a game board — a small plot on a scrap of ground
between the houses of a town, cut out and set down on the game's own road.

SIZE ON SCREEN — READ THIS FIRST, it decides the whole picture. On the map this
object is drawn about 70 pixels wide. Anything smaller than a twelfth of the
picture's width disappears at that size. So the picture is built around the three
bean-pole A-frames: they carry the silhouette. The soil, the ridges and the rows
are the ground the poles stand in, not the subject.

WHAT IS BIG AND WHAT IS SMALL:
- The A-frames stand about TWO THIRDS of the picture's height, and each one is
  about a quarter of the picture's width across its feet.
- The cabbages in the FRONT row are big and simple: about an eighth of the
  picture's width each, four or five of them across the front, drawn as plain
  round leafy balls.
- Everything further back — the other rows, the onion and carrot tops — is small
  and reads as texture on the soil. Do not spend detail there.
- The soil patch itself is a SHALLOW BAND across the bottom of the picture,
  about a third of the picture's height and no more. It is a flat surface seen
  from above, NOT a block of earth: it has no front face, no side face and no
  visible depth. The only timber in the picture is the bean poles.

SQUARE-ON — CRITICAL. The plot is seen STRAIGHT FROM THE FRONT and only a little
from above, exactly as the town's houses are drawn: the soil shows as a shallow
band with its ridges running level across the picture. NO corner points at you. NO receding side edge
is visible. This is NOT an isometric view, NOT a three-quarter view, NOT a
perspective view.

THE FOOT — the near edge of the soil patch runs across the bottom of the
picture, and that is where the game sets the plot on the ground. Draw nothing
below it: no grass, no paving, no path, no shadow, and above all no cut edge of
earth.

SHAPE — low and wide: about twice as wide as it is tall, counting the poles. It
stands beside the houses of the town and must read as smaller and lower than they
are. The plot is the only object in the picture, sits in the middle, and touches
no edge — leave clear white on all four sides.

THE BACKGROUND is PURE FLAT WHITE, #FFFFFF, with NO texture, NO shading, NO
gradient, NO speckle, NO grey, NO drop shadow. The white is deleted and the game
draws its own road under the plot, so the edge against the white must be hard
and clean.

NO LIGHT DIRECTION — nothing has a bright side and a dark side, and nothing casts
a shadow onto anything else: no shadow of a pole on the soil, none along the
near edge. Shading stays inside a surface: the soil is a shade darker than the
poles, and that is all. Read at thumbnail size: a few large legible shapes, no
fine detail, no picked-out leaves.
