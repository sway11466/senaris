DESIGN — the town's kitchen garden. This block is repeated word for word in every
slot of this skin, so that the little plot on the board and the two bands in the
battle window are plainly the same garden.

- The plot is FLAT GROUND: turned soil, dark brown #4E3B2A, level with the road
  around it. It has NO THICKNESS — no slab of earth, no raised bed, no cut face
  of soil along the front, no step down to the road, and no edging of any kind
  (no plank, no board, no kerb, no stones, no box, no frame).
- The soil is worked into low ridges running left to right.
- Bean poles: A-frames of peeled sticks, brown #6E5A42, two poles crossed and
  tied near the top, with bean vines winding up them so each reads as a leafy
  triangle rather than as bare sticks.
- Vegetables grow in the rows: round cabbages, onion tops, carrot tops, in a
  muted mid green #7E9660, one kind to a ridge. Bare soil shows between them.
- NEVER: a fence, a hedge, a wall, a gate, a trellis panel, a shed, a scarecrow,
  a well, a water butt, a cart, tools, baskets, flowers, fruit, people, animals.
  A fence is its own terrain in this game and must never appear here.

SUBJECT: The BEAN POLES of that garden, standing BEHIND the fighting in a battle
window — a WIDE BAND of backdrop, seen close and from the front at eye level. The
soldiers fight among these poles: the rows of vegetables are drawn in the other
slot, down at their feet, and this picture is what stands up behind them.

WHAT IS IN IT — FIVE or SIX A-frames of bean poles in a line across the band,
each one leaning at its own slight angle, at slightly different heights and
spacings, never evenly spaced. EVERY frame is thickly covered in bean vines and
leaves, from the ground to the tie at the top: each one reads as a leafy
triangle. There are gaps of clear white BETWEEN the frames so the row never
becomes a solid hedge, but no frame anywhere in the picture is bare. NEVER draw
naked sticks, dead vines, wilted leaves or an empty frame.

THE SOIL at the foot of the poles is a shallow strip, no more than a fifth of the
band's height, with one or two ridge lines and a few small vegetable tops. It is
FLAT GROUND seen from a little above: NO thickness, NO slab of earth, NO cut face
along the front or the side, NO step, NO platform. The poles stand on it with
their feet all at about the same level, the way posts stand on level ground.

THE TWO ENDS ARE DIFFERENT.
- LEFT end: the row of poles runs straight off the edge of the picture and is cut
  by it. Nothing tapers and nothing is rounded off there.
- RIGHT end: the garden has to finish inside the picture. The last two A-frames
  are drawn SMALLER and set a little higher up in the band, as things do when they
  stand further away — but they are just as leafy as the others, and the soil
  under them narrows and ends in a soft irregular edge. Smaller and further away,
  NEVER balder, NEVER dying, NEVER a straight cut edge of earth. Do NOT stop the
  row in mid-air with a raw broken end.

SHAPE AND PLACE IN THE FRAME — the picture is square. The band fills the BOTTOM
HALF of it, edge to edge: twice as wide as it is tall. In a 1024-pixel picture
the garden occupies the bottom 512 pixels and the full 1024 of width. The tallest
A-frame reaches near the top of that half; the top half of the picture is empty
white.

THE BOTTOM EDGE — THIS WAS WRONG LAST TIME, so read it twice. The soil runs all
the way down to the very bottom edge of the picture and is CUT by it. There is NO
strip of white under the garden, NO gap, NO margin, and the band does not float
in the middle of the frame: its lowest pixel is the bottom row of the picture.
Draw nothing below the soil — no path, no paving, no grass, no cast shadow. The
soldiers stand in front of this band and their heads overlap its lower part.

KEEP IT READABLE AT SMALL SIZE — the poles are thin, so draw them boldly: clear
dark outlines, flat leaf masses with no veins and no highlights, no fine twigs,
no small scattered detail. A few large legible shapes.

NO LIGHT DIRECTION — nothing has a bright side and a dark side, and nothing casts
a shadow onto anything else: no shadow of one frame on another, none on the soil.
Shading stays inside a surface. This band is mirrored left to right when the game
lays it down, so a baked light direction would end up pointing both ways in the
same window.

THE BACKGROUND — everything above and between the poles is PURE FLAT WHITE,
#FFFFFF, with NO texture, NO shading, NO gradient, NO sky, NO clouds. This white
is deleted, so every outline against it must be hard, clean and crisp — including
the gaps between the frames and the holes between the vines. Nothing in the
garden may be white or near-white.
