DESIGN — the town's kitchen garden. This block is repeated word for word in every
slot of this skin, so that the little plot on the board and the band in the
battle window are plainly the same garden.

- The plot is FLAT GROUND: turned soil, dark brown #4E3B2A, level with the road
  around it. It has NO THICKNESS — no slab of earth, no raised bed, no cut face
  of soil along the front, no step down to the road, and no edging of any kind
  (no plank, no board, no kerb, no stones, no box, no frame).
- The soil is worked into low ridges running left to right.
- Bean poles: A-frames of peeled sticks, brown #6E5A42, two poles crossed and
  tied near the top, with bean vines winding up them so each reads as a leafy
  triangle rather than as bare sticks.
- Vegetables grow in the rows: round cabbages, onion tops, carrot tops, in a
  muted mid green #7E9660, one kind to a ridge. Bare soil shows between them.
- NEVER: a fence, a hedge, a wall, a gate, a trellis panel, a shed, a scarecrow,
  a well, a water butt, a cart, tools, baskets, flowers, fruit, people, animals.
  A fence is its own terrain in this game and must never appear here.

SUBJECT: A NEW composition, not a crop or an extension of the map picture: a LOW
BAND of that kitchen garden lying in the FOREGROUND of a battle scene, seen from
the front at eye level, for a wide landscape picture (16:9). The soldiers stand
just behind it and their feet are hidden by it, so this band is the bottom edge
of the battle window — a frame at the very front, not scenery in the distance.

LAYOUT — in a 1792 x 1024 picture the garden is a VERY SHALLOW strip about 170
pixels tall resting on the BOTTOM edge, running from about 120 pixels in from
the left edge to about 120 pixels in from the right edge. Shallow is the whole
point: this is only the NEAR EDGE of the plot — the first row of vegetables and
a sliver of soil behind them — not a field. The rest of the garden is drawn in
the other slots, and the game draws its own ground behind this band. The margins at both ends, and
everything above the strip, stay EMPTY WHITE. NOTHING is cut by the frame:
nothing touches the left, right or top edge, and nothing continues off the
picture. The band simply starts and stops, with the smallest plants at its two
ends.

WHAT IS IN IT — ONE row and no more:
- SIX to EIGHT round cabbages, big and simple, sitting on the bottom edge and cut
  by it, each one about a tenth of the strip's length across and nearly as tall
  as the whole strip. They are the picture.
- Behind them, only a narrow band of turned soil with ONE ridge line in it,
  perhaps a couple of small onion or carrot tops poking up between the cabbages.
  Its top is a soft slightly irregular line where the soil ends.
- NO second row, NO third row, NO rows receding into the distance, NO field
  stretching back, NO horizon. Drawing depth here would fight with the ground the
  game already draws behind this band.
- NO bean poles in this picture. The poles of this same garden stand BEHIND the
  fighters and are drawn in the other slot, at their proper height; a stunted
  A-frame down here would contradict them. Nothing in this band rises above the
  top of the strip.

KEEP IT QUIET — this band sits under the fighters' feet and must not compete
with them. Draw it darker and simpler than the map piece: leaves as flat masses
with no veins and no highlights, soil as flat tone with only the ridge lines
picked out, and no small scattered detail. A silhouette that reads in one glance.

NO LIGHT DIRECTION — nothing has a bright side and a dark side, and nothing
casts a shadow onto anything else: no shadow of the poles on the soil, none
under a cabbage. Shading stays inside a surface. This band is mirrored left to
right when the game lays it down, so a baked light direction would end up
pointing both ways in the same window.

THE BACKGROUND — everything outside the band is PURE FLAT WHITE, #FFFFFF, with
NO texture, NO shading, NO gradient, NO speckle, NO grey, NO drop shadow. The
white is deleted, so the top edge of the band must be a hard, clean, crisp line
against it. Nothing in the garden may be white or near-white.
