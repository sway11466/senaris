SUBJECT: A NEW composition, not a crop or extension of any earlier picture:
a long, DENSE heap of collapsed graveyard masonry lying across the ground in
the FOREGROUND of a battle scene, seen from the front at eye level, for a wide
landscape picture (16:9). Draw it as ONE SELF-CONTAINED object: the heap has a
clear left end and a clear right end, both well inside the picture, with wide
empty white on either side. The most important rule: NOTHING is cut by the
frame — the heap tapers down to loose chunks at both ends and STOPS, and no
stone touches the left edge, the right edge or the top edge.

LAYOUT — in a 1792 x 1024 picture, the heap lies in a LOW STRIP about 240
pixels tall resting on the bottom edge, about 1150 pixels long and CENTRED: its
left end stops about 320 pixels in from the left edge and its right end stops
about 320 pixels in from the right edge. Those margins stay EMPTY WHITE, as
does everything above the strip. The stone may rest on the bottom edge. No
stone may rise above the strip.

THE RUBBLE — this is a COLLAPSED BURIAL VAULT, not a few stones on the grass:
squared ashlar blocks, broken roof slabs and cracked lintels piled two or three
deep along the whole length of the heap, wedged against and on top of each
other at every angle, with smaller chips and grit filling every gap. Jammed in
among them at wrong angles are TWO OR THREE old GRAVESTONES — slabs with
rounded tops, one snapped in half — so the heap plainly reads as a graveyard's.
There is NO bare ground anywhere along the heap, NO path through it, NO flat
footing — a soldier on foot could not cross it; only something that flies could
pass. The heap is uneven: higher in two or three places where big blocks stack,
lower between them, ragged along the top, never a smooth bank and never a row.
Fallen stone only: NOT a cliff, NOT a standing wall, NOT an intact building, no
arch left standing, no column, no doorway.

STYLE — the same clean stylized cel-shaded look as the game's terrain and unit
art: bold flat shading, clear dark outlines, a mature, slightly muted, limited
color palette (NOT bright saturated, NOT painterly photorealism). Because this
rubble sits CLOSE to the camera, in front of the soldiers' feet, draw it a
shade DARKER and SIMPLER than the map version — big plain shapes, little inner
detail, dim in shadow — so it frames the scene rather than competing with the
figures behind it.

COLOR — dull grey building stone, hex #6C695E (darker than the map version
because it is in shadow), each piece a slightly different flat tone between
#7C7970 and #5A5750. The gravestones caught in the heap are a paler grey-green,
hex #8E8A76 — the same stone as the grave markers in the game's other
graveyard pictures. A little dull grey lichen is welcome; NO bright green moss, NO
crystals, NO glow, NO water, NO strong red, blue or green anywhere.

THE BACKGROUND — everything that is not stone is PURE FLAT WHITE, #FFFFFF: NO
texture, NO shading, NO gradient, NO ground, NO grass, NO soil, NO drop shadow
on the white. This white is going to be deleted and the strip laid over the
game's own battle scene, so every outline must be a hard, clean, crisp edge
against it. Nothing in the stone may be white or near-white.

LIGHT — from directly above only, short soft shadows that fall only on the
stone itself; NOTHING casts a shadow onto the white, and NO light from the left
or the right.

Nothing else: no creature, no people, no bones, no skulls, no coffins, no urns,
no flowers, no candles, no plants, no torches, no treasure, no lettering and no
inscriptions. NO glow, NO mist, NO magic. NO border, NO frame around the
picture itself, NO vignette. Landscape 16:9.
