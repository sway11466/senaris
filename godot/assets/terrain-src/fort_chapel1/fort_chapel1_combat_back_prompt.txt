SUBJECT: The same small stone CHAPEL as the map piece, seen CLOSE — a WIDE
BAND of backdrop that stands behind the fighting in a battle window. It is not
a single object floating in the middle of the frame; it is a stretch of the
graveyard seen near, running off the LEFT edge of the picture.

WHAT IS IN IT — the chapel stands a little LEFT of the middle of the band, the
tallest thing in the picture. It is one small country chapel of pale grey
dressed stone: a single hall — one nave, no side wings, no cloister, no tower
block — with a steep pitched roof, standing noticeably taller than it is wide.
On the roof ridge above the front wall sits a small open bell-gable, and on top
of that a plain stone CROSS: simple, thick, clearly upright, standing straight
and whole — NOT tilted, NOT broken, NOT fallen, NOT desecrated. In the front
wall is a tall arched TIMBER DOOR, plainly centred and firmly SHUT: a solid
boarded door with heavy iron hinges, no opening, no gap, no darkness showing
through — nothing comes out of here. Two or three worn steps run down from it.
High on either side of the door is one narrow arched window with plain leaded
glass, dark and unlit.

THE ROOF is the second most important shape after the cross: the whole of it
must be plainly visible from this angle, big and simple, because the game
recolours it to show who holds the chapel.

AROUND IT — old grave markers stand in the ground on both sides of the chapel:
plain weathered slabs with rounded tops, short crosses with thick stubby arms,
snapped stubs, each leaning at its own angle, scattered irregularly and never
in a row. They are much lower than the chapel — the tallest reaches about a
quarter of its height.

THE TWO ENDS ARE DIFFERENT — this is the most important thing.
- LEFT end: the graveyard runs straight off the edge of the picture and is cut
  by it. The markers nearest the left edge are cut through by the frame.
  Nothing tapers, nothing ends, nothing is rounded off there.
- RIGHT end: the graveyard has to finish inside the picture. Finish it
  honestly: past the chapel the markers get smaller and lower as the ground
  recedes away from the viewer, and the last of them fade out. Do NOT stop the
  row in mid-air with a raw broken end, and do NOT put a wall, fence, hedge,
  kerb or gate there — there is no boundary anywhere in this picture.

SHAPE AND PLACE IN THE FRAME — the picture is square. The band fills the BOTTOM
HALF of it, edge to edge: about twice as wide as it is tall. In a 1024-pixel
picture the chapel is about 430 pixels tall including its cross, and everything
else is lower. The band touches the left edge, the right edge and the bottom
edge. Leave a little clear space above the cross; nothing rises higher than it.

THE BOTTOM EDGE — the walls, the steps and the grave markers are cut flat by
the bottom edge of the picture. Draw nothing below them: no grass, no soil, no
path, no paving, no ground, no cast shadow. The stonework must run all the way
down to the very bottom edge and be cut by it — no strip of background along
the bottom. Soldiers will stand in front of this and their heads will overlap
the bottom of it.

VIEWING ANGLE — seen from the front and slightly above, much closer than the
map piece: the front wall, the shut door, the steps and the bell-gable are all
clearly readable, and the whole roof is still in view above them.

COLOR — the chapel's stone is pale grey with almost no colour in it, neither
warm nor cool: base tone #B1AC9B where the light catches it, #7A776C in shadow.
The grave markers are a weathered grey-green, hex #8A8676. Only the door
timber, its hinges and the roof timbers are weathered brown. The ROOF is plain
grey slate, as colourless as the walls — base tone #97948A, shadowed slopes
#7C796F, catch-light #B7B2A4. Match those numbers: this chapel and the map
piece must read as the same building. NO ochre, NO warm tan, and NO strong red,
blue or green anywhere: those colours are what marks who holds the place, and
nobody holds it in this picture.

THE BACKGROUND — everything above the roofline is PURE FLAT WHITE, #FFFFFF,
with NO texture, NO shading, NO gradient, NO sky, NO clouds. This white is
going to be deleted, so the roofline and the cross must be a hard, clean, crisp
edge against it. Nothing in the picture may be white or near-white.

NOTHING ELSE — no people, no animals, no birds, no bones, no skulls, no open
graves, no flowers, no candles, no lanterns, no banners, no signage, no smoke,
no fire, no lettering and no inscriptions. The mood is quiet, weathered and
kept, NOT ruined and NOT haunted: NO glow of any kind, NO lit windows, NO mist,
NO floating lights, NO magic circles.

This is a near view, so a little more detail than the map piece is welcome —
the courses of the stonework, the boards of the door, the ridge line of the
roof — but keep it in bold flat shapes, in the same clean stylized cel-shaded
look as the game's terrain and unit art, with no fine texture that would fight
with the figures standing in front of it. Square 1:1.
