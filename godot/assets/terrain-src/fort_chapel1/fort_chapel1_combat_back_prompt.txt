SUBJECT: One small rural stone chapel — a backdrop that stands behind the
fighting in a battle window.

VIEWING ANGLE — seen from slightly above and slightly in front. The side
wall with its windows faces the viewer almost square-on, only barely
angled: the front gable wall with the door is just visible at the RIGHT
end of the building, turned away from the viewer. THE DEPTH IS SHALLOW —
this is a backdrop band standing close behind fighting figures, so keep
the foreshortening minimal: the building must NOT drive diagonally into
the distance, and its far end must NOT shrink much. The whole roof is
fully in view above the walls — the game recolours the roof to show who
holds the chapel, so no part of the roof may be hidden. Nothing pokes
above the cross of the bell-cote; leave a little clear space over it.

THE BOTTOM EDGE — the walls of the chapel are cut flat by the bottom edge
of the picture. Draw nothing below them: no ground, no grass, no path, no
soil, no wall, no fence, no steps, no cast shadow. The walls must run all
the way down to the very bottom edge and be cut by it — no strip of
background along the bottom. Soldiers will stand in front of the chapel
and their heads will overlap the bottom of its walls.

SHAPE AND PLACE IN THE FRAME — the picture is square. The chapel fills
the BOTTOM HALF of it, standing centered, its walls cut by the bottom
edge. Everything around it is background (see below).

DESIGN (follow exactly, every count and position):
- One rectangular nave only. Strictly symmetrical building. NO tower, NO
  transept, NO side annex, NO porch, NO chimney.
- One steep gabled saddle roof covering the whole nave.
- Front gable wall: one pointed-arch double door centered at ground level,
  and one round window centered directly above the door. Nothing else on
  this wall.
- On the roof ridge, at its front end: one small stone bell-cote (an open
  stone frame) holding one bell, topped by one cross.
- Each side wall: three pointed-arch windows evenly spaced, and four
  buttresses at equal intervals — one at each corner of the wall and one
  between each pair of windows.
- Walls: rough-cut stone, base colour #B1AC9B, shaded side #7A776C.
- Roof: flat stone slabs in one uniform colour — base #97948A, shaded
  areas #7C796F, lit areas #B7B2A4. Every slab is the SAME colour as its
  neighbours — do NOT alternate lighter and darker slabs, NO patchwork,
  NO stripes, NO checker pattern, NO decorative bands, and NO part of the
  roof darker than #7C796F. Shading may follow only the slope of the
  roof, never individual slabs.

THE BACKGROUND — everything around the chapel is PURE FLAT WHITE,
#FFFFFF, with NO texture, NO shading, NO gradient, NO sky, NO clouds.
This white is going to be deleted, so every outer edge must be a hard,
clean, crisp edge against it. Nothing in the picture may be white or
near-white.

NOTHING ELSE — no people, no animals, no trees, no graves, no carts, no
flags, no smoke, no birds, no wall, no fence.

This is a near view, so a little more detail than the map piece is
welcome — the courses of the stonework, the boards of the door, the ridge
line of the roof — but keep it in bold flat shapes, with no fine texture
that would fight with the figures standing in front of it.

Square 1:1.
