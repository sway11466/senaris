STYLE: A side-on rock-face texture for a fantasy tactics game, in the same clean
stylized cel-shaded look as the game's terrain and unit art: bold flat shading, a
mature, slightly muted, limited color palette (NOT bright saturated, NOT
painterly photorealism). Viewed straight from the FRONT, face-on — a flat lay
with NO perspective, NO horizon, NO sky, NO ground line, NO floor. The texture
fills the ENTIRE square frame edge to edge, with NO border, NO frame, NO
vignette, and no single big focal object. It must tile SEAMLESSLY LEFT TO RIGHT:
the left and right edges continue into each other. CRITICAL: vary the rock
RANDOMLY and UNEVENLY — never a repeated motif, never the same shape twice,
never in rows of equal height. Avoid large, high-contrast patches (they repeat
visibly when tiled); keep large-scale color even and low-contrast. Square 1:1.

SUBJECT: The cut face of a mountainside where the bedrock has been sheared away,
seen face-on. The rock is LAYERED: broad horizontal beds stacked on top of each
other, each bed a different thickness, their edges running roughly sideways
across the face but wavering, pinching out and picking up again — never a stack
of even stripes and never straight parallel lines. Where a bed juts out it
leaves a blunt lip with a darker recess under it; where one has crumbled the
face steps back into a shallow scoop. Short vertical joints cut across a few of
the beds and stop, but the relief is clearly HORIZONTAL overall — the opposite
of a face that split downward.

Pale warm grey stone, hex #B4A895 — use this exact stone colour and keep it
consistent. Do NOT drift cooler, bluer, more purple or more saturated than it,
and do NOT make it much darker: this is the same pale rock as the mountain's
upper surface, seen in section, only a shade deeper. Shade every plane as ONE
FLAT TONE — a lighter bed face, a mid face, a darker recess under each lip —
flat cel shading of the rock's own faces, NOT a cast shadow and NOT a light
direction, and no face near-black.

Read at thumbnail size: the whole face is only a narrow strip on screen, so keep
the beds CHUNKY and few — about five to eight beds from top to bottom of the
frame — with no fine detail that would disappear. Spread the relief evenly from
top to bottom, with no single large feature that sits at one height only.

NO separate stones, NO cobbles, NO boulders set side by side, NO joints between
pieces, nothing that reads as built or assembled. NOT masonry — NO stacked
bricks, NO mortar lines, NO worked edges, NO tool marks. NO vegetation of any
kind — NO grass, NO moss, NO lichen, NO trees, NO soil, NO snow. NO scree or
rubble pile at the bottom, NO talus slope, NO top surface, NO ground, NO objects
of any kind.
