DESIGN — a dwarven mine's ore dump. This block is repeated word for word in
every slot of this skin, so that the pile on the board and the band in the
battle window are plainly the same place.

- The ORE: rough, angular chunks of dark grey-brown rock, hex #5C554D, fist-
  sized to head-sized, with pale dull pewter streaks and specks on the broken
  faces, hex #B8B2A4 — never bright silver, never gold.
- The MINE CART: a squat wooden box on four small iron wheels, weathered dull-
  brown planks, hex #6E5238, bound with dark iron straps, heaped over the brim
  with the same ore.
- The PICK: a miner's pick with a long timber handle in the same brown and a
  dark iron head, always standing with the head UP so it reads at a glance.
- Everything muted, in the game's clean stylized cel-shaded look: bold flat
  shading, clear dark outlines, a limited palette. NO strong red, blue or
  green anywhere. NO gold, NO gems, NO lanterns, NO fire, NO people.

SUBJECT: The ore dump standing BEHIND the fighting in a battle window — a
WIDE BAND of backdrop, seen close and from the front at eye level. The
soldiers fight in front of this; loose ore at their feet is drawn in another
slot, and this picture is what stands up behind them.

WHAT IS IN IT, from left to right, with clear white gaps between the things:
a MINE CART heaped with ore, standing on a short length of iron rail on
wooden sleepers; a tall heap of ore beside it; a second MINE CART, this one
tipped up on its end, spilling ore; a wooden RACK of tools — two picks and a
shovel, heads up, leaning in a row; and a low heap of ore at the end. The two
carts are the biggest things in the picture and read first. Vary the heights
and spacing; nothing evenly spaced, nothing in a neat row.

THE GROUND at the foot of the things is a shallow strip of the same ore-strewn
earth, no more than a fifth of the band's height, FLAT and seen from a little
above: NO thickness, NO slab, NO cut face along the front, NO step, NO
platform. Everything stands on it with its feet at about the same level.

THE TWO ENDS ARE DIFFERENT.
- LEFT end: the rail and the ground run straight off the edge of the picture
  and are cut by it. Nothing tapers there.
- RIGHT end: the dump finishes inside the picture. The last heap is drawn
  SMALLER and set a little higher in the band, as things do when they stand
  further away, and the ground under it narrows and ends in a soft irregular
  edge. Do NOT stop the band with a raw broken end.

SHAPE AND PLACE IN THE FRAME — the picture is square. The band fills the
BOTTOM HALF of it, edge to edge: twice as wide as it is tall. In a 1024-pixel
picture the dump occupies the bottom 512 pixels and the full 1024 of width.
The tallest thing (the tipped cart or the tool rack) reaches near the top of
that half; the top half of the picture is empty white.

THE BOTTOM EDGE — the ground runs all the way down to the very bottom edge of
the picture and is CUT by it. There is NO strip of white under the band, NO
gap, NO margin: its lowest pixel is the bottom row of the picture. Draw
nothing below the ground. The soldiers stand in front of this band and their
heads overlap its lower part.

KEEP IT READABLE AT SMALL SIZE — big plain shapes, clear dark outlines, flat
colour areas, no fine planking detail, no small scattered stones.

NO LIGHT DIRECTION — nothing has a bright side and a dark side, and nothing
casts a shadow onto anything else. Shading stays inside a surface. This band
is mirrored left to right when the game lays it down, so a baked light
direction would end up pointing both ways in the same window.

THE BACKGROUND — everything above and between the things is PURE FLAT WHITE,
#FFFFFF, with NO texture, NO shading, NO gradient, NO sky, NO rock wall, NO
mine entrance. This white is deleted, so every outline against it must be
hard, clean and crisp — including the gaps between the carts and the rack.
Nothing in the dump may be white or near-white.
