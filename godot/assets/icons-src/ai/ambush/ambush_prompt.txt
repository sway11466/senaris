SUBJECT: An ambush mark — ONE set steel jaw trap seen from the SIDE at eye level, left-right symmetrical about a vertical axis. The trap is open and waiting: two thick jaw bars are spread wide apart and lie low, rising outward from the middle to the left and to the right, so together they make one broad shallow V — a mouth held open, ready to snap shut upward. Along the top edge of each bar stand four or five big blunt teeth pointing up and slightly inward; the teeth are few and large, never a fine saw edge. In the middle, between the two bars, sits a small round pressure plate lifted just above the base. At each outer end, one blunt square spring block finishes the bar. Below the whole thing runs a low flat base bar tying the two halves together. NO chain, NO stake, NO rope, NO ground line, NO leaves, NO grass. Heavy and crude iron, not machinery: no gears, no cogs, no clock face, no ring or circle enclosing it — seen from the side it must never read as a wheel. DRAW IT BIG: the mark spans the full width of the square canvas, the two spring blocks touching or nearly touching the left and right edges, the tooth tips reaching close to the top edge and the base bar close to the bottom edge. Leave only a thin margin, a few percent of the canvas — do NOT float a small mark in a large empty background. Warm amber-gold, one light tone with at most one slightly darker tone of the same amber inside the jaw bars, hard flat edges — a branding-iron mark, NOT rendered polished steel, NO shine, NO rust, NO glow. Pure black background (single flat color, for easy cutout).
