SUBJECT: A withdrawal mark — ONE heavy war boomerang seen flat from above, two arms of equal length meeting at one rounded elbow. The elbow sits in the UPPER LEFT of the frame. One arm runs from the elbow to the RIGHT, rising slightly as it goes, and ends near the right edge in the upper part of the frame. The other arm runs from the elbow DOWN, leaning slightly to the left as it goes, and ends near the bottom edge in the left part of the frame. The bend is shallow and wide open: the two arms spread at an obtuse angle of about 110 to 120 degrees, wider than a right angle, so the elbow is a soft broad curve rather than a sharp corner. Each arm is wide and flat with a gentle curve, nearly the same thickness along its length, ending in a blunt rounded tip — NOT pointed, NOT a blade. Each arm carries ONE broad band painted straight across it, square to the arm, set about a quarter of the arm's length in from its tip: two bands in all, one per arm. The bands are wide — about as wide as the arm is thick — and are filled in the slightly darker amber tone, so they stand out clearly against the light amber of the arm even at tiny size. Apart from these two bands the boomerang is plain: no carving, no other stripes, no dots, no binding, no wood grain. It is a thrown weapon that flies out and comes back to the hand: heavy, crude and brutish, a club for breaking bones, NOT a toy, NOT a sleek sports boomerang. Nothing else is drawn: NO hand, NO motion lines, NO curved flight path, NO arrow, NO second boomerang. It must never read as a bird, a chevron arrow, a check mark or a crescent moon. DRAW IT BIG: the elbow close to the top-left corner, the right-hand arm's tip close to the right edge, the lower arm's tip close to the bottom edge. Leave only a thin margin, a few percent of the canvas — do NOT float a small mark in a large empty background. Warm amber-gold, the arms in one light tone and the two bands in one slightly darker tone of the same amber, hard flat edges — a branding-iron mark, NOT rendered wood, NO shine, NO glow. Pure black background, a single flat color with no wood grain (for easy cutout).
