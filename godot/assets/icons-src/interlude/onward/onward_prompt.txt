SUBJECT: A war horn — the signal horn that calls a company straight back into the fight before it can rest. ONE curved horn lies across the square canvas as a thick crescent: the narrow end at the LOWER-LEFT corner, the body sweeping right and upward in one smooth curve that bulges toward the lower-right, the wide open bell at the UPPER-RIGHT corner with its mouth facing up and to the right. The horn is about a fifth of the canvas thick at the bell and tapers evenly to the narrow end. A small dull brass mouthpiece caps the narrow end, a plain brass band rings the bell at its mouth, and a dark brown leather thong hangs from the horn in one simple loop near the mouthpiece, so it reads as a horn that is blown and carried, not a drinking cup. The horn is deep amber-brown, lighter along the upper edge of the curve and shaded clearly darker along its lower edge and inside the bell so the shape does not wash out against pale paper. The surface is smooth: no carving, no engraving, no ridges, no bands other than the two brass ones. NO person, NO hands, NO sound lines, NO ground, NO second horn, NO liquid. DRAW IT BIG: the narrow end reaches the lower-left corner and the bell reaches the upper-right corner of the square canvas. Leave only a thin margin, a few percent of the canvas.
