SUBJECT: An end-of-turn mark — ONE hourglass seen flat from the front, symmetrical about a vertical axis and about a horizontal axis. Two flat wide bars, one across the top and one across the bottom, each a plain thick rectangle with square ends. Between them the glass: an upper bulb and a lower bulb of the same size, each a rounded triangle that is wide against its bar and narrows toward the middle, where the two meet at one narrow waist; the waist is a clear pinch, about a fifth of the bar's width, so the outline reads as two triangles tip to tip. The glass is a solid shape in the lighter amber; inside the lower bulb a heap of sand in the slightly darker amber fills the bottom half of that bulb, and a small heap of the same darker amber sits in the bottom of the upper bulb. NO frame posts at the sides, NO stream of falling sand, NO stand, NO decoration on the bars. A plain, sturdy timekeeper — the shape of a turn coming to its end. DRAW IT BIG: the top bar touches or nearly touches the top edge of the square canvas, the bottom bar the bottom edge, and both bars span the full width from the left edge to the right edge. Leave only a thin margin, a few percent of the canvas — do NOT float a small mark in a large empty background. Warm amber-gold, one light tone plus one slightly darker tone of the same amber, hard flat edges — a branding-iron mark, NOT rendered glass or metal, NO shine, NO transparency, NO glow. Pure black background (single flat color, for easy cutout).
