SUBJECT: Victory art for "Undead Rush". Everyone stands on the same ground, the same distance from the camera.

The same old town graveyard as the cover art — the same crooked headstones, the same bare dead tree, the same rusty iron fence — but the night is over. Warm golden dawn pours through the branches, the fog and the purple haze burned away. No enemies, no undead standing: only a few harmless old bones, a tattered rag and a rusted broken blade lying quietly in the grass.

Four heroes celebrate together, smiling with relief. The young knight grins, bareheaded in steel-blue plate, his large kite shield lowered to rest on the ground and his sword raised. The priest beside him — a man in layered steel-blue and off-white vestments with gold trim, bareheaded, his cross-topped rod cradled in one arm, a hand pressed to his chest in thanks. The witch laughs with her staff shouldered and its crystal dark at last — semi-long brown hair under a wide-brimmed dark purple hat, black bodice and short black skirt. With them a paladin who arrived for the last battle: bareheaded, ornate steel plate, a steel-blue and off-white cloak bearing a bold gold cross, his straight cross-hilted sword at rest.

NOT night. No gore.
