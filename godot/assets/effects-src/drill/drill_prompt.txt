SUBJECT: A drilling impact mark — the moment a spinning rock drill grinds into its target, drawn as a flat graphic stamp: a bold SPIRAL whorl of three or four thick, chunky curved bands winding inward in one direction, like the grooves a boring drill cuts, forming a roughly circular ring. The bands are wide at the outside and taper as they curl inward, each ending in a blunt point; they must stay THICK, not hairline, so the spiral still reads when the whole graphic is shrunk very small. The middle of the spiral is EMPTY: plain background color showing through, NO filled core, NO disc, NO starburst in the center — the spiral frames a hollow hole. Dull steel-grey bands with a darker grey shadow tone, two tones only, hard flat edges. This is a flat graphic stamp, NOT a soft glow, NOT sparks, NOT rock chips, NOT debris or particles, NOT motion blur. The spiral fills the frame with only a thin margin. No drill, no machine, no ground. Pure black background (single flat color, for easy cutout).
