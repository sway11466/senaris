SUBJECT: A chibi PALADIN calling down a divine judgment, with a cleric and a priest praying quietly behind him. The paladin is unmistakably the main subject — large, centered, and forward; the other two are small supporting figures set back and to the sides, partly turned away, clearly secondary. Do not give the three equal weight.

THE PALADIN (center, foreground, dominant): a young man with short brown hair, bare-headed, in silver plate armour trimmed with gold, a gold cross on the breastplate and on each pauldron, a white surcoat hanging from the waist, and a blue-grey cape. He holds a long straight sword with a gold cross-shaped hilt. He stands braced with both feet planted, one arm raised, thrusting the sword STRAIGHT UP toward the sky in a single clean vertical line, head tilted back to follow the blade. His eyes are closed and his lips are parted — he is speaking the oath, not shouting. Calm and resolute, never snarling.

THE BAND OF LIGHT (the spectacle): the sword's blade blazes, and from its TIP a BAND OF LIGHT is FIRED straight up into the sky, running clean off the top edge of the frame. This is light LEAVING the sword, not light falling on the paladin — the whole picture must read as him sending power away, never as him being blessed or spotlit. So:

- The band starts NARROW at the sword tip, only about as wide as the blade, and WIDENS as it climbs, so it is at its widest where it exits the top of the frame. Do NOT draw a cone that is wide at the ground and narrows toward the sky — that reads as a spotlight shining down on him, which is exactly wrong.
- The band does NOT touch the ground and does NOT reach below the sword. There is NO ring or pool of light around the paladin's feet, NO glowing circle on the earth beneath him, NO light cone enclosing his body. The ground around him stays dark.
- Streaks, slender motes and thin gold cross-glyphs inside the band all travel clearly UPWARD and outward, stretched as they go, so the flow direction is unmistakable.
- Light it accordingly: the paladin is edge-lit from his own blade — a hard warm rim down the raised arm and the near side of the armour — while the rest of him stays in the storm's shadow.

Far away on the horizon, off to one side and small, a single slender shaft of the same golden light comes DOWN out of the torn clouds and strikes the distant ground — that is where the judgment lands. Keep it thin and far, a distant answer to the band, so the eye follows the light up, across the sky, and down onto a target far from the paladin himself.

THE TWO SUPPORTS (small, behind, restrained): a cleric and a priest kneel on one knee some distance behind the paladin, one on each side, heads bowed low, eyes closed, hands clasped in prayer — reverent and still, taking up little space. The cleric is a young man in a blue-grey hooded robe with the HOOD UP, a cream off-white tabard down the front, brown belt and boots, a small pale stone cross held between his hands. The priest is a young woman in a gold-trimmed blue-grey veil over a white coif and a blue-grey robe over a cream underlayer with a gold cross embroidered at the chest, her cross-topped staff laid across her knees or planted beside her. Neither of them raises a weapon, neither looks at the viewer, and neither competes with the paladin for attention.

The three figures read clearly against the light — do not let the band wash them out. NO enemies, NO corpses, NO named characters, NO hex grid, NO UI. Background: a dark storm-heavy sky over a low horizon of empty battlefield, the clouds torn open directly above the sword where the band punches through, and torn open again far away where the distant shaft comes down. Keep it simple so the paladin and his outgoing band of light dominate.
