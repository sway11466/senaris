SUBJECT: A prey mark — ONE split shield seen flat from the front, left-right symmetrical about a vertical axis. Draw an unmistakable heater shield: a straight flat top edge, square shoulders at both upper corners, straight sides running down and curving in to meet at one blunt point at the bottom centre. Keep that outline whole and unbroken — no bite taken out of it, no missing chunk, no ragged edges, no notches on the rim. The damage is a single split THROUGH the face: one wide cleft runs from the top edge down to the bottom point, leaning slightly so it is not perfectly vertical, cutting the shield into two halves that still sit in place with a narrow gap of background showing between them. That gap is bold and open — roughly a tenth of the shield's width — never a hairline crack, and there is only one: NO branching cracks, NO spider web, NO dents, NO scratches, NO rivets, NO straps, NO boss, NO heraldry, NO emblem, NO pattern on the face. Nothing else is drawn: NO sword, NO axe, NO weapon, NO hand, NO ground. The shield is plain, thick and crude, and it has failed — armour that no longer holds, the mark of a soft target the enemy will pick out. DRAW IT BIG: the shield fills the square canvas, its top edge touching or nearly touching the top edge of the frame, its shoulders reaching the left and right edges, its point close to the bottom edge. Leave only a thin margin, a few percent of the canvas — do NOT float a small mark in a large empty background. Warm amber-gold, one light tone with at most one slightly darker tone of the same amber along the broken inner edges, hard flat edges — a branding-iron mark, NOT rendered metal, NO shine, NO rust, NO glow. Pure black background (single flat color, for easy cutout).
