SUBJECT: A treant — an ancient tree that walks, the forest's living wall. It is a PLANT, not an animal and not a person in a costume: a single thick gnarled tree trunk as the body, two heavy branch arms, and a knot of thick roots for feet that grip the ground. NO fur, NO scales, NO animal limbs, NO clothing, NO armour, NO shield, NO helmet, NO weapon, NO tools or ornaments of any kind — nothing man-made anywhere on it. Chibi proportions: a huge blunt trunk-head over a squat, extremely wide body and short thick root legs, so it reads as a heavy, immovable block. Its toughness is shown as mass and thickness: deep cracked slabs of bark, big knots and burls, a doubled trunk thickness at the shoulders, and a broad flat-topped silhouette — nothing sharp, nothing spiky. The bark is sooty grey-brown and charcoal, weathered and dry, NOT green: only a few small tufts of dry, dull, brown-green leaves at the shoulders and the ends of the branch arms, small in area and low in saturation, so the piece never blends into the green forest ground tiles it stands on. Its face is part of the trunk — a calm, rounded face formed by two hollows and a wide seam of a mouth, kind and patient rather than angry, snarling or menacing; deep inside the eye hollows a dull red ember glows, and a few small dull-red berries hang among the leaves — the red hollows and berries are the ONLY colour on the piece. Ancient, mossy in patches, quietly imposing — a thing that blocks the road rather than chases you. Keep the mature, muted, limited palette: sooty grey-brown bark, charcoal shadow, dry dull leaves, and the dull red eyes as the single accent.
