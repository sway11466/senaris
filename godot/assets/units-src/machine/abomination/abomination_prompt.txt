SUBJECT: A half-beast, half-machine abomination — the boss of the ancient ruins, a failed creation spat out by an ancient furnace that fused beasts and war machines together; it is neither a beast nor a machine, and has NO human part. It is a huge, tall, two-legged MACHINE frame with adult proportions, NOT chibi: the SAME brass-and-rusted-iron build as the gear soldier (dark rusted iron plates held together by big rivets, worn brass bands, exposed joints, a few loose cables and pipes). Here and there, dark grey FLESH has grown over the machine: thick, smooth, rubbery bundles of sinew-like tissue, like tendons or roots, wrapping around the joints, the arms, the legs and the chest and hanging down in places between the iron plates. The flesh is dark charcoal grey with a faint cool sheen — NOT red, NOT raw meat, NO blood, NO veins, NO exposed organs. Seen head-on and facing the viewer. The head is split down the middle: the viewer's LEFT half is a lump of the same dark grey flesh, and the viewer's RIGHT half is an iron machine head with ONE round eye lens glowing red — the only red and the only glow on it; no mouth, no teeth, no other eyes. Both arms are machine arms with flesh wrapped around them in places, and both end in big iron claws instead of hands, each with three long hooked talons, the two claws matching left and right. Both legs are thick machine legs with flesh wrapped around them in places, standing firmly on heavy iron feet. No magic glow, no aura, no tank, no crest and no ornaments. Big, heavy and imposing — clearly larger and grander than the gear soldier, a boss. Colours: mostly dark rust-brown iron, darker than a brown cave floor, with worn brass-yellow bands and rivets catching the light to lift its outline, the dark charcoal-grey flesh, a dark outline, and the single red eye. Keep the mature, muted, limited palette.

NOTE: the STYLE block's instructions about a strictly symmetrical, neutral stance are relaxed for this piece only as far as described above: the split head differs left and right; everything else stays straight-on, front-facing and centered.
