SUBJECT: A NEW composition, not a crop or extension of any earlier picture:
a short row of goblin plunder lying on the floor, drawn as ONE SELF-CONTAINED
GROUP that sits well inside the frame with wide empty white margins on BOTH
sides. The most important rule of this picture: NOTHING is cut by the frame.
Every barrel, crate and sack is drawn COMPLETE and WHOLE with white space
beyond it; no object touches the left edge, the right edge or the top edge;
nothing continues off the picture.

The plunder is the same battered kind as the game's map version — weathered
wooden barrels with rusty hoops, smashed slatted crates, patched and grimy
burlap sacks, crude and mistreated — SCATTERED LOOSELY along the floor in the
FOREGROUND of a battle scene, seen from the front at eye level, for a wide
landscape picture (16:9).

LAYOUT — in a 1792 x 1024 picture, the clutter lies in a LOW STRIP about 240
pixels tall resting on the bottom edge, split into TWO SEPARATE GROUPS with
EMPTY FLOOR between them: a LEFT group about 600 pixels wide whose outer end
stops about 80 pixels in from the left edge, and a RIGHT group about 600
pixels wide whose outer end stops about 80 pixels in from the right edge. The
middle of the picture — roughly the central 400 pixels — holds NOTHING but
white. The two groups are different from each other (different mix of
barrels, crates and sacks, different poses), not mirror copies. The margins at
both ends and everything above the strip stay EMPTY WHITE. The objects may
rest on the bottom edge. No object may rise above the strip.

THE CLUTTER — spread out unevenly, not a single heap: a barrel on its side here,
an upright barrel there, a crate tipped on its corner, two or three sacks
slumped against each other, a burst sack with a little grain spilled, a snapped
board and a loose lid, a gnawed bone, an empty gap of bare floor, then more. Vary the spacing: some pieces touching, some
apart, NEVER evenly spaced, NEVER in a row. Keep the objects LOW — barrels on
their sides and slumped sacks read best; anything standing tall must stay
within the strip.

STYLE — the same clean stylized cel-shaded look as the game's terrain and unit
art: bold flat shading, clear dark outlines, a mature, slightly muted, limited
color palette (NOT bright saturated, NOT painterly photorealism). Because this
clutter sits CLOSE to the camera, in front of the soldiers' feet, draw it a
shade DARKER and SIMPLER than the previous image — big plain shapes, little
inner detail, dim in shadow — so it frames the scene rather than competing
with the figures behind it.

COLOR — weathered mid-brown timber, hex #6B4E32 (darker than before because it
is in shadow); dull dark iron hoops; undyed grey-tan burlap, hex #8F816A; the
spilled grain a pale dull straw colour, never bright yellow. NO gold, NO coins,
NO treasure, NO weapons, NO strong red, blue or green anywhere.

THE BACKGROUND — everything that is not an object is PURE FLAT WHITE, #FFFFFF:
NO texture, NO shading, NO gradient, NO ground, NO floor, NO cave wall, NO drop
shadow on the white. This white is going to be deleted and the strip laid over
the game's own battle scene, so every outline must be a hard, clean, crisp edge
against it. Nothing in the objects may be white or near-white.

LIGHT — from directly above only, short soft shadows that fall only on the
objects themselves; NOTHING casts a shadow onto the white, and NO light from
the left or the right.

Nothing else: no goblin, no creature, no people, no animals, no rats, no
torches, no fire, no lettering, no signs. NO border, NO frame around the
picture itself, NO vignette. Landscape 16:9.
