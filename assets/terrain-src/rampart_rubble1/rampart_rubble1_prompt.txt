SUBJECT: The wreckage of a graveyard crypt that has caved in completely, seen from straight above. Two things are mixed together in the heap: the crypt's own masonry, collapsed so utterly that NOTHING of the building's shape survives, and the grave markers around it, toppled and broken apart.

The crypt pieces are thick flat slabs and plain squared blocks — roof slabs, wall stones, floor slabs, a cracked lintel, chunks of pale mortar still stuck to them. NOT ONE of them is still joined to another: no corner, no course, no standing fragment of wall, no doorway, no arch, no step, no roofline, nothing that lets you tell which way the building faced or where it stood. It reads as a pile of stone, not as a ruin you could reconstruct.

The grave markers are the pieces that say "graveyard": broken headstones of the same kind that stand in the burial ground nearby — round-topped slabs, square shouldered slabs, a stone cross with short thick arms now snapped off at one arm, flat grave slabs cracked in two. Snapped off at the base and lying flat or wedged at an angle, never upright, never planted. A few are whole enough to still read as a headstone; the rest are just fragments. No carving, no inscriptions, no writing, no names, no symbols on any of them.

STRICTLY FORBIDDEN — classical or temple architecture of any kind: NO columns, NO column drums, NO fluted stone, NO capitals, NO bases, NO cornices, NO moulded or profiled edges, NO decorative carving, NO pediments. This is a humble country burial ground, not a temple. Also NO cliff face, NO natural boulders, NO mountain crags, NO gravel bed, NO coffins, NO open graves. A few old bones and a skull or two may lie among the stones — it is a burial ground — but keep them FEW, SMALL and as pale as the stone, tucked in the gaps; this is not a boneyard and they must never become a feature of the picture.

It must read as IMPASSABLE. The pieces lie jumbled and tilted at every angle, stacked two or three deep so the heap is visibly thick, wedged against each other with no flat footing anywhere. There is NO path, NO gap, NO clear lane and NO bare ground large enough to walk through; a person on foot would have to climb, and it looks like it would shift underfoot.

FILL THE FRAME. The wreckage runs right off all four edges of the square and is cut by them — pieces are chopped in half at the border on every side. There is NO empty ground margin around the heap, NO band of grass framing it, NO border, NO frame, NO vignette, NO darker edge of any kind. Do NOT build a single mound, cone or blob at the middle; the density is roughly the same everywhere across the square, just with the clumping varied.

Ground shows only in the narrow gaps BETWEEN the stones — thin irregular slivers, never a clearing. That ground is the same dry burial-ground earth as the neighbouring tiles: drained yellowed-olive dead grass, hex #B2B28E, over pale dusty earth, hex #A89877. Keep those two exactly; do NOT push the ground to orange, rust, desert sand or fresh green.

The stone is a dull weathered grey-tan, hex #B9B2A4 — the same stone as the graveyard's markers, clearly paler than the grass but never white. Each piece takes a slightly different flat tone somewhere between #C4BEB0 and #A79E90, so the pieces separate from one another by tone alone.

CRITICAL — NO LIGHT DIRECTION. The tile gets rotated and mirrored on the board, so nothing may imply where the light comes from: NO cast shadows, NO drop shadows between pieces, NO lit top faces paired with dark side faces, NO highlight or gradient running the same way across the frame. Every face of every block is a single flat tone, whichever way that face points. Give the pieces their form with clean outlines and flat per-piece tone only. For the same reason this picture has no top and no bottom — it must read correctly upside down and mirrored.

Piece size varies: the largest slabs and headstone fragments are about one fifth of the frame height (roughly 200px in a 1000px image), most are smaller, down to fist-sized chips, scattered irregularly — dense clumps in some places, looser scatter in others, NEVER evenly spaced, NEVER in rows, NEVER radiating from the centre.

CRITICAL, and the easiest thing to get wrong: the picture must stay LIGHT OVERALL — squint at it and it should sit close to the dry grassland around it, never a dark grey or brown picture. Dark cracks, dark hollows and dark outlines are what drag the average down, so keep every dark accent both FEW and THIN. If in doubt, use fewer of them and make them paler.
