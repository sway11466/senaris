STYLE: A map tile texture for a fantasy tactics game, in the same clean
stylized cel-shaded look as the game's terrain and unit art: bold flat shading,
clear dark outlines, a mature, slightly muted, limited color palette (NOT bright
saturated, NOT painterly photorealism). Drawn like the world map of a classic 2D
role-playing game, seen from DIRECTLY OVERHEAD — flat, no perspective, no
horizon, no sky, no depth, nothing standing up. Read at thumbnail size: a few
large legible shapes, no fine detail. Square 1:1.

SUBJECT: The flat TOP of the thick stone parapet of a fortress wall — the heavy
capping blocks you look straight down onto at the wall's edge, seen from
directly above. Just the capping stones, filling the whole picture.

FILL THE FRAME — IMPORTANT: the capping stones cover the ENTIRE square, edge to
edge, corner to corner. This is a texture swatch, not a scene and not an object.
NO ground, NO grass, NO walkway, NO floor below, NO background, NO sky, NO
border, NO frame, NO vignette, NO rounded corners, NO drop off the side. The
stonework runs off all four sides. Nothing stands on it and nothing lies on it.

SOLID TOP SURFACE ONLY — CRITICAL: NO battlements, NO merlons, NO crenellations,
NO notches, NO gaps, NO embrasures, NO arrow slits, NO loopholes, NO openings of
any kind. Any notch would point one fixed way, and this texture is laid on walls
running in every direction, so it must have NO direction at all. For the same
reason: NO rows, NO courses, NO grid, NO brickwork or running bond, NO joint
line that runs straight across more than about a fifth of the frame, and no
grain or drift running any one way across the picture. The picture must read
correctly turned upside down.

THE STONES: big squared capping blocks fitted tightly against each other, each a
slightly different size and shape — mostly four-sided, some five-sided, wandering
joints between them, a few smaller blocks wedged in. A block is roughly a sixth
of the frame across — plainly BIGGER and heavier than the walkway paving slabs
that border this stone inside the same map tile, so the two read apart at a
glance. Cut and dressed by a mason, but weathered: corners rounded off, faces
faintly pitted, an occasional chipped edge. NO tool marks, NO chisel gouges, NO
carved shapes, NO inscriptions.

THE JOINTS: the gaps between blocks are THIN and DARK — narrow dark lines, a
little darker than the stone itself. NO white or pale joints, NO wide mortar
beds, NO bright grout.

COLOR: cool grey granite, roughly hex #8E9498 — a faintly blue-grey stone. It
must read as the DARKER of the two stones that meet inside one map tile: clearly
darker than the wall's pale walkway paving (#A9AEB0) that it borders, but never
near-black and never a dark charcoal picture overall. Squint at it and it should
still read as mid-grey stone. Plainly different from the graveyard rubble's warm
grey-tan (#B9B2A4) and from the game's grass (#B4C6A0). Each block is a slightly
different shade of that grey, some a touch warmer, some a touch cooler,
scattered unevenly so the variation never falls into a pattern. NO block may be
white or near-white. NO moss, NO lichen, NO grass, NO soil, NO puddles, NO snow,
NO leaves, NO rubble, NO cracks big enough to notice, NO props of any kind.

Lit dimly and evenly from straight above, with NO cast shadow at all and no
light direction: this is a flat top surface and throws no shadows. Shading stays
inside each block — very slight, just enough to tell one block from the next.
