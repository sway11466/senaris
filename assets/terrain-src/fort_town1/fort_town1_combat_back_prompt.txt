SUBJECT: The same stone TOWN as the board piece, drawn as a WIDE BAND of
backdrop that fills the defender's half of the battle window. It is not a
single object standing in the middle of the frame — it is a stretch of the town
seen close, running off the left edge of the picture.

Across the whole width runs the town's drystone wall of rough grey fieldstone,
with one simple timber gate, shut, a little left of centre. Behind and above the
wall the town rises: squat cottages of the same rough grey fieldstone, packed
shoulder to shoulder at varied sizes and slightly varied angles — never in a row
— with one taller hall standing head and shoulders above the rest. Every
building has a steep pitched roof of slate laid over timber beams. Nothing
pokes above the top edge; leave a little clear space over the highest roof.

THE TWO ENDS ARE DIFFERENT — this is the most important thing.
- LEFT end: the wall and the town run straight off the edge of the picture and
  are cut by it. Nothing tapers, nothing ends, nothing is rounded off there.
- RIGHT end: the town has to finish inside the picture. Finish it honestly: the
  wall turns a corner and runs away from the viewer, the cottages behind it get
  smaller and lower as they recede, and the last of them fades out. Do NOT stop
  the wall in mid-air with a raw broken end.

SHAPE — a wide band, about twice as wide as it is tall. Fill the frame side to
side; do not leave white margins at the left or right.

THE BOTTOM EDGE — the base of the wall is cut flat by the bottom edge of the
picture. Draw nothing below it: no ground, no grass, no soil, no paving, no cast
shadow. Soldiers will stand in front of the wall and their heads will overlap
its base.

VIEWING ANGLE — seen from a slight high three-quarter angle, as if looking down
onto the battlefield from above and in front, but much closer than a map: the
wall faces, the gate and the front walls of the nearest cottages are clearly
readable, and the roofs are still fully in view above them.

COLOR — the stone is plain grey with almost no colour in it, neither warm nor
cool; the slate roofs are the same neutral grey. Only the gate, the doors and
the roof timbers are weathered brown. NO ochre, NO warm tan, and NO strong red,
blue or green anywhere.

NOTHING ELSE — no people, no animals, no carts, no barrels, no flags, no
signage, no smoke, no fire, no birds, no sky.

This is a near view, so a little more detail than the map piece is welcome — the
courses of the stonework, the boards of the gate, the ridge line of a roof — but
keep it in bold flat shapes, with no fine texture that would fight with the
figures standing in front of it.
