STYLE: A side-on rock-face texture for a fantasy tactics game, in the same clean
stylized cel-shaded look as the game's terrain and unit art: bold flat shading, a
mature, slightly muted, limited color palette (NOT bright saturated, NOT
painterly photorealism). Viewed straight from the FRONT, face-on — a flat lay
with NO perspective, NO horizon, NO sky, NO ground line, NO floor. The texture
fills the ENTIRE square frame edge to edge, with NO border, NO frame, NO
vignette, and no single big focal object. It must tile SEAMLESSLY LEFT TO RIGHT:
the left and right edges continue into each other. CRITICAL: vary the rock
RANDOMLY and UNEVENLY — never a repeated motif, never the same shape twice,
never in columns of equal width. Avoid large, high-contrast patches (they repeat
visibly when tiled); keep large-scale color even and low-contrast. Square 1:1.

SUBJECT: The sheer face of a crag that no one can climb, seen face-on. The rock
has SPLIT DOWNWARD into tall wedges and slabs that lean against each other, their
arrises sharp, with deep narrow clefts running down between them. The clefts
wander, branch and pinch out; they are never straight, never parallel, and never
evenly spaced. Here and there a wedge has sheared away and left a blunt overhang
with a dark undercut beneath it. A few short cross-fractures cut sideways and
stop, but the relief is clearly VERTICAL overall — this is the same shattered
rock as the crag's upper surface, seen from the side.

The fracturing reaches the TOP EDGE of the frame evenly, with no skyline, no
crest, no summit and no single tall spike breaking the top: the face has been cut
off flat where the frame ends. The bottom edge is cut off the same way — NO
ground, NO scree, NO rubble pile, NO talus slope, nothing that reads as the
bottom of the cliff.

Mid warm grey stone, hex #85796A — use this exact stone colour and keep it
consistent. Do NOT drift cooler, bluer, more purple or more saturated than it,
and do NOT make it much lighter: this is the same rock as the crag's upper
surface, seen in section, a shade deeper. Shade every plane as ONE FLAT TONE — a
lighter face, a mid face, a dark cleft — flat cel shading of the rock's own faces,
NOT a cast shadow and NOT a light direction, and no face near-black.

Read at thumbnail size: the whole face is only a narrow strip on screen, so keep
the wedges CHUNKY and few — about six to ten of them across the width of the
frame — with no fine detail that would disappear. Spread the relief evenly from
top to bottom, with no single large feature that sits at one height only.

NO separate stones, NO cobbles, NO boulders set side by side, nothing that reads
as built or assembled. NOT masonry — NO stacked bricks, NO mortar lines, NO
worked edges, NO tool marks, NO carved shapes. NO vegetation of any kind — NO
grass, NO moss, NO lichen, NO trees, NO soil, NO snow. NO water, NO caves, NO
openings, NO objects of any kind.
