STYLE: A map tile object for a fantasy tactics game, in the same clean stylized
cel-shaded look as the game's terrain and unit art: bold flat shading, clear
dark outlines, a mature, slightly muted, limited color palette (NOT bright
saturated, NOT painterly photorealism). Drawn like the world map of a classic 2D
role-playing game, seen from DIRECTLY OVERHEAD — a flat lay with NO perspective,
NO isometric or three-quarter view, no horizon, no sky, no depth, nothing
standing up and nothing leaning towards the viewer. Read at thumbnail size: a
few large legible shapes, no fine detail. Square 1:1.

SUBJECT: ONE block of a town — eight to ten small houses standing in two or
three short terraces with lanes between them — seen from straight above, sitting
alone on a plain white background so that it can be cut out and laid over the
game's own ground.

VIEWPOINT — CRITICAL: you are looking straight DOWN at the roofs. Draw the ROOF
SURFACES, not the front of the buildings. Each house is a simple gabled roof: an
oblong split down its middle by a ridge line into two sloping halves, one half a
shade lighter than the other. NO walls, NO windows, NO doors, NO facades,
nothing standing up. A few small square chimneys seen from above are fine.

SIZE AND PLACEMENT — the block is a single clump in the MIDDLE of the picture.
In a 1024-pixel picture it is about 660 pixels wide and 500 pixels tall, leaving
about 180 pixels of white to its left and right and about 260 pixels of white
above and below. It must NOT touch any edge and must NOT be cut by any edge.
There is exactly ONE block: no second cluster, no stray hut out on its own, no
road, no wall, no fence around it.

THE LANES — MOST IMPORTANT: people have to be able to walk to every house.
Narrow lanes thread through the block, about a twelfth of the block's width
across (roughly 55 pixels in a 1024-pixel picture). At least two of them open
from the outside of the block and run right into the middle, and one small
square opens out where they meet. EVERY house has a lane along at least one of
its sides. NO house is sealed in on all four sides by other houses.

THE LANES ARE WHITE — draw the lanes in exactly the same pure white as the
background, not as a paved or earthen street. They are cut out along with the
background, and the game draws its own ground through them, so a lane must join
the outside white without a break.

OUTLINES — each house is outlined on its own. Do NOT draw an outline around the
block as a whole. Where a lane opens to the outside, NOTHING closes it off: no
line, no kerb, no bar, no gate, no dotted line across the mouth of the lane. A
lane is empty white that joins the white outside without a single stroke
crossing it. No line may run through white where there is no building.

THE CLUMP — within a terrace the houses stand shoulder to shoulder and share
walls, all squared up to the same lane, the way a row of town houses does. The
terraces themselves sit at slightly different angles to each other. The OUTLINE
of the block is IRREGULAR: houses of different depths, so the edge steps in and
out and no side of the block is one long straight wall. Overall it is a chunky
mass, wider than it is tall — NOT a perfect rectangle, NOT a circle, NOT a star,
NOT a ring closed all the way round a courtyard.

COLOR — muted and limited. Most roofs are weathered terracotta tile, hex
#A9705C; a good number are grey slate, hex #8C8A82; one or two are straw thatch,
hex #B39A6E. Mix the three kinds across the block so no side of it is all one
colour. Each roof is a slightly different shade of its own material. NO roof may
be white, near-white or near-black — the white is reserved for the background.

THE BACKGROUND — everything outside the block is PURE FLAT WHITE, #FFFFFF, with
NO texture, NO shading, NO gradient, NO speckles, NO grey, NO grass, NO soil, NO
street, NO drop shadow. This white is going to be deleted and the game will draw
its own ground there, so the outside of the block must be a hard, clean, crisp
edge against it.

NO SHADOWS — this rule was broken last time, so read it twice. NO house has a
dark edge along one side and a pale edge along the other. NO house drops a
shadow into a lane, onto the white, or onto the house beside it. There is NO
light direction in this picture at all: a roof looks exactly the same whichever
way it is turned. This tile is rotated and mirrored when it is laid on the map,
so a baked light direction would end up pointing several different ways across
the board. Shading
stays inside each roof: the two slopes of a gable differ slightly, and that is
all.

Nothing else: no people, no animals, no carts, no market stalls, no laundry, no
smoke, no trees, no gardens, no wells, no city wall, no tower, no church, no
gate, no signboards, no lettering. NO border, NO frame around the picture
itself, NO vignette, NO caption, NO logo.
