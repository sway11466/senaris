STYLE: A top-down ground-terrain tile for a fantasy tactics game, in the same
clean stylized cel-shaded look as the game's unit art: bold flat shading, a
mature, slightly muted, limited color palette (NOT bright saturated, NOT
painterly photorealism). Viewed straight from directly overhead — a flat lay
with NO perspective, NO horizon, NO sky. The texture fills the ENTIRE square
frame edge to edge, with NO border, NO frame, NO vignette, and no single big
focal object. CRITICAL: scatter any small details (grass tufts, pebbles)
RANDOMLY and UNEVENLY — a few loose clusters here, bare empty gaps there —
NEVER evenly spaced, NEVER in rows or a regular grid. Avoid large,
high-contrast patches or blotches (they read as a big feature and repeat
visibly when tiled); keep large-scale color even and low-contrast. Square 1:1.

SUBJECT: Neglected, dying grassland on the edge of a town, the ground that runs
out to an old graveyard. Coarse grass that has gone dry and yellow, thinning
out, with tired bare soil showing faintly through it here and there. This is
first and foremost a QUIET, PLAIN, ALMOST EMPTY field of dry grass: the colour
carries it, not the detail. Most of the frame is bare dry grass with nothing on
it at all. The soil shows in only a handful of small openings, each about as
wide as a tuft of grass, scattered irregularly with wide empty gaps between —
NOT dozens of them, NOT a speckle all over the frame, and NOT a few big bald
patches either.

The dead grass is the base color, hex #B2B28E — a drained, yellowed olive. The
soil showing through is a PALE dusty earth, hex #A89877, only a little darker
than the grass. These read as the same family as the game's healthy grassland
(#B4C6A0) with the life drained out of it: nearly the same lightness, less
green, more ochre. Do NOT push it to orange, rust or desert sand.

CRITICAL, and the easiest thing to get wrong: the picture must stay LIGHT
OVERALL. Squint at it and it should be only a little darker than a field of
green grass — never a brown or earthy picture. Dark brown soil, dark stones and
dark shadows are what drag the average down, so keep every dark accent both
FEW and SMALL. If in doubt, use fewer of them and make them paler.

Loose objects on the ground are RARE. In the whole frame there are only a
handful of them in total: a few small stones half sunk into the soil, two or
three dry fallen twigs, a clump of grass that has collapsed and lies flat and
matted, and perhaps one low scrubby weed. That is all — resist adding more.
They are pale and dusty, never dark, and they sit in loose clusters with large
stretches of plain grass in between. NO carpet of pebbles, NO litter of leaves
and sticks spread evenly over the frame.

The mood is quiet neglect — ground nobody tends any more — NOT scorched, NOT
poisoned, NOT magical. NO cracked dried mud, NO desert, NO ash, NO burn marks,
NO green glow, NO mist. NO gravestones, NO headstones, NO bones, NO skulls, NO
crosses, NO fences, NO paths, NO tracks, NO buildings, NO water, NO flowers.
Nothing that reads as a single object placed on the tile.
