STYLE: A top-down ground-terrain tile for a fantasy tactics game, in the same
clean stylized cel-shaded look as the game's unit art: bold flat shading, a
mature, slightly muted, limited color palette (NOT bright saturated, NOT
painterly photorealism). Viewed straight from directly overhead — a flat lay
with NO perspective, NO horizon, NO sky. The texture fills the ENTIRE square
frame edge to edge, with NO border, NO frame, NO vignette, and no single big
focal object. CRITICAL: scatter any small details (grass tufts, pebbles)
RANDOMLY and UNEVENLY — a few loose clusters here, bare empty gaps there —
NEVER evenly spaced, NEVER in rows or a regular grid. Avoid large,
high-contrast patches or blotches (they read as a big feature and repeat
visibly when tiled); keep large-scale color even and low-contrast. Square 1:1.

SUBJECT: An old graveyard gone to seed, on the edge of a town. The ground is
exactly the same neglected dying grassland as the graveyard's approach tile: dry
yellowed grass, hex #B2B28E, with pale dusty soil, hex #A89877, showing through
in only a handful of small openings. Use these exact colors and keep the ground
even and low-contrast — the two tiles must look like one continuous field.

Standing in that grass are OLD GRAVE MARKERS. The ground stays flat and seen
from straight above, but the markers themselves are drawn STANDING UPRIGHT and
facing the bottom of the frame, the way buildings are drawn on the world map of
a classic 2D role-playing game — so you see the face of each one, and each casts
a short soft shadow onto the grass beside it. They must match the grave markers
drawn around the mausoleum on the neighbouring tile.

Vary the shapes, and use a DIFFERENT one for each marker: a slab with a rounded
top, a squared-off upright slab, a stone CROSS with short thick arms, and a
broken stub whose top has snapped off. Give some of them a slight lean, each at
its own angle, so none of them stand quite straight.

There are only FOUR or FIVE markers in the whole frame. Scatter them irregularly
— two close together, one off on its own, wide empty grass between — NEVER
evenly spaced, NEVER in a row, NEVER in a grid, and NEVER near the middle in a
way that makes one of them the subject of the picture. Keep them well away from
the four edges.

Keep them SMALL and QUIET: each one only a little taller than a clump of grass,
dull mid-grey stone that is clearly paler than the grass but NOT white and NOT
black-outlined, with soft low-contrast shading. The cross has thick, stubby arms
so it still reads as a cross when the picture is shrunk — NOT a thin spindly
one. They should read as graves at a glance without any one of them punching out
of the picture, because this tile is laid down many times side by side and a
loud marker would repeat visibly.

Nothing else stands up from the ground. NO building of any kind, NO tomb, NO
mausoleum, NO hut, NO wall, NO doorway, NO opening, NO entrance, NO steps, NO
archway, NO fence, NO gate, NO path — anything that reads as a structure or an
opening belongs to a different tile and must not appear here. NO crosses, NO
religious symbols, NO statues, NO urns, NO flowers, NO candles, NO bones, NO
skulls, NO freshly dug earth, NO open graves, NO mounds. NO glow, NO mist, NO
green or purple light, NO magic.

The mood is quiet neglect — a burial ground nobody visits any more.
