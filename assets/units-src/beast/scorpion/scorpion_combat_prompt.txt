POSE (melee): A dynamic battle-ready action pose — weapon raised or mid-strike, weight forward, leaning into the blow as the character presses toward the enemy on the left.

SUBJECT: The SAME scorpion as the scorpion board piece — a huge armoured cave scorpion (same sooty black shell, same earth-brown joints, same small red eyes; this must read as the exact same creature). It is a WILD ANIMAL, an arthropod: one low, wide body of thick chitin plates, eight jointed legs, two big heavy pincers, and a segmented tail. NO fur, NO feathers, NO human limbs, NO clothing, NO armour straps, NO saddle, NO collar, NO weapon and NO tools or ornaments of any kind. Its weapon is its own body: seen from the side, it braces low on its eight legs with both blunt pincers thrown forward and open toward the enemy on the LEFT, while the segmented tail is cocked high over its back and whipping down and forward so the single black sting drives at that side — the sting strike is the main action and must read instantly. Its toughness is shown as mass and thickness: overlapping slabs of shell, a domed back, and pincers so thick they look like a pair of blunt anvils; the only sharp thing on the whole piece is the sting at the tail tip. Colours: sooty black and dark grey-brown shell, a clear step darker than pale grey-brown cave rock, with earth-brown at the leg joints and leg tips; the claws and the sting are black; a small row of simple round red eyes on the front of the shell is the only colour. NO glow, NO green or purple venom shine, NO dripping, NO smoke. Same sooty-and-ash treatment and same black claw finish as the griffon. This piece is not humanoid, so the style's head-count proportions, face, hands and feet instructions do not apply to it; instead keep the pincers and the tail thick and oversized so they still read at tiny sizes, and keep all eight legs planted on the ground (it is a ground unit, never floating).
