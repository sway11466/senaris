SUBJECT: A ghost — the flying spirit of the undead horde. A pale, faintly see-through wraith: thin drooping shoulders, long thin arms, and a hollow sunken face whose eyes are lit by a COLD BLUE-GREEN glow — the SAME cold blue-green glow as the skeleton's eye sockets (the sign that the corpse moves). It wears the remains of a burial shroud, once-proper grave cloth now rotted, the frayed hem trailing away beneath it. It has NO legs and NO feet: below the waist the body dissolves into a wispy, tapering mist tail. It carries NO weapon; both thin hands are raised in front of the chest, mirrored left and right at the same height with the same bend — perfectly symmetrical. IMPORTANT — IT FLOATS: the whole figure hovers clearly ABOVE the ground with an obvious gap of empty air under the mist tail, and a small soft oval shadow is cast on the ground below it. Reading as airborne at a glance is essential. IMPORTANT — the body is square to the camera and completely frontal: head, shoulders and hips all face the viewer straight on, not turned or leaning to either side. Translucent in feel — the mist tail and the hem fade out softly — but keep the body itself solid enough that the silhouette still reads clearly at a small size; do NOT make it so transparent that the shape disappears. Sorrowful and cold rather than angry — not comical, not cute. Keep the mature, muted, limited palette: pale blue-grey shroud and mist, faint rot brown at the hem, with the cold blue-green glow ONLY in the eyes (small and contained). NOT bright cartoon colors.
